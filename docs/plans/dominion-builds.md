# Dominion: four finished builds (proposal)

**Status:** proposal, 2026-10-14, for the user to react to. The user asked: *"please draft 3-4 dominion finished builds and your rationale for how it should play, and why it's fun/fulfills some power fantasy."* The method is the card-design skill's "Finished builds" section: about eleven endgame chairs in four parts (foundation, engine, finishers, flex), with more candidates than fit so no build can be forced. Once the user approves, the builds move into the skill's deck map (`.claude/skills/card-design/references/deck-maps.md`).

**How cards are marked:**
- *new*: one of the user's picks that is not in the game yet;
- *proposed*: suggested in conversation, not picked;
- *changing*: the user's revision, not built yet;
- *deferred*: in the data but not dealt.

Tiers are the sheet's: 0 to IV.

**What the four have in common.** Each build pushes one quantity that the player can grow by choice and that several cards read:
- **The garrison state:** soldiers stationed in towns, up to three per town.
- **The endless host:** units gained and units lost.
- **The steppe raiders:** mounted units and pillage.
- **The conqueror:** towns taken and held.

That shared quantity is what makes a build more than a pile of good cards: each card pays more because the others raise what it reads.

**The war costs ruled on 2026-10-14 give each build a different relationship to war:**
- *Unrest:* a town taken by force works only once it is pacified.
- *Weariness:* net losses cost happiness.
- *Ranks:* units that fight grow stronger.

So the four builds feel different in play, not only in their numbers.

---

## 1. The garrison state

**The fantasy.** Hadrian's Wall, the Great Wall, the Theodosian walls of Constantinople, the soldier-farmers of the Chinese frontier. An empire whose soldiers are its civil service. It never needs to attack. Invaders break on its walls and go home weary, and behind the walls it grows rich and builds the Magnum Opus.

**Why it's fun**
- **The power fantasy:** invulnerability, and wealth that peace brings. The bots' armies come and fail.
- **The moments:**
  - a siege broken at your walls, while the attacker's weariness climbs, because the losses are theirs;
  - a frontier citadel claiming a mountain pass;
  - the Opus city behind the Praetorian Guard.
- **The decision:** how many soldiers to station in each town. A second and a third soldier pay more but cost upkeep. Upkeep relief is the enabler, which is the build's internal synergy.
- **Low tedium:** stationed units stand still. This is the "fewer pieces, placed once" build.

**How it plays**
- **Age 1:** garrison every town and build walls. Cards pay per garrisoned unit (food, happiness, culture).
- **Age 2:** soldier-farmers. Walled and garrisoned towns feed and build, and upkeep relief lets each town hold a second soldier.
- **Age 3:** two or three soldiers per town, and citadels on the frontier. Defensive lines make an attack on you costly.
- **Age 4:** each garrisoned soldier pays science and culture, and the Opus city is a fortress.

**How it wins:** it builds the Magnum Opus behind its walls. It is Dominion's peaceful route to victory.

**The eleven chairs**

| Part | Cards |
|---|---|
| Foundation | Sword and Plough (0, *changing*), Martial Law (0, *changing*), The Farming Garrisons (0, *new*) |
| Engine | The Soldier-Farmers (I), Walled Towns (I, *new*), Stockpiles (II), Standing Garrisons (III), Defensive Lines (III) |
| Finishers | The Military Frontier (IV), The Praetorian Guard (IV) |
| Flex | The Standing Army (0, rare: garrisoned units cost no upkeep, which is the enabler) |

**Other candidates:**
- from the sheet: "+1 authority per garrisoned unit" (0, *new*), "+4 strength in your own territory" (0, *new*), March Discipline (I), The Citadel Works (II), The Marshalate (IV);
- in conversation: The Scythian Defence (I, *proposed*) and The Teutoburg Wood (I, *proposed*);
- shared: Examinations of State (II, Magisterial) and The Road Garrisons (II, Expansionist).

**Gives up:** conquest and tempo. Its production goes into soldiers who never leave home.
**Partners:** Expansionist (settle wide behind the walls: Marcher Lords, The Road Garrisons) and Magisterial (Examinations of State).
**Leader:** Emperor Taizong (melee strength; garrisoned towns pay happiness and culture).

**What it needs**
- **A moment.** Nothing pays when an invader dies at your walls. A card like "each enemy unit killed in your territory grants culture equal to its strength" turns being attacked into a reward. The Scythian Defence does part of this job.
- **Engine cards that read soldiers, not towns.** Sword and Plough pays "each city with a garrison", which is the same for every Dominion player. Read "each garrisoned unit, at most three per town" instead, as The Military Frontier and Standing Garrisons already do, so that stationing a second soldier is a choice.
- **A ruling.** This build pays for army held at peace, which the tempo ruling avoids. The cap of three per town makes it scale with towns, not army size. Is that acceptable?

## 2. The endless host

**The fantasy.** Xerxes' army that drank rivers dry; the tumens; the einherjar of Valhalla who die each day and rise again. An empire that turns its people into soldiers and its soldiers into knowledge and faith, and never runs out. *"They have more men than we have arrows."*

**Why it's fun**
- **The power fantasy:** inexhaustible numbers. Every soldier raised pays, and every soldier lost pays again, so you can throw armies at anything. It is the build for the user's "feeding units to die".
- **The moments:**
  - a levy turns a citizen into a swordsman on the spot;
  - a wave of spearmen dies at a camp and the science bar jumps;
  - the grower ticks up every turn;
  - at the end, the army marches into the capital and builds the Magnum Opus with its own hands (The Legions' Labour).
- **The decisions:** spend a citizen or keep him; which fights to feed. Barbarians are the free sparring partner: losses to them cost no weariness, and the user's "camps spawn faster near you" card turns that into an engine.
- **A closed loop with Devotion:** units die, which pays faith (The Funeral Games); faith buys units (The Sacred Host); new units pay again (Muster Rites).

**How it plays**
- **Age 1:** every unit gained pays (the grower, Muster Rites). Feed barbarian camps.
- **Age 2:** levies and free units (The Call to the Spear, The Open Muster). Gaining pays science (The Art of War), and fighting pays science and culture on both sides (Songs of the Fallen).
- **Age 3:** the host lives off the land abroad with no upkeep, and Total War throws it forward.
- **Age 4:** armouries raise units every ten turns, and the army is spent into the Opus.

**How it wins:** science and culture from gaining and losing units drive research and drafts. At the end the army itself becomes the Magnum Opus's production.

**The eleven chairs**

| Part | Cards |
|---|---|
| Foundation | the grower: +1 science and +1 culture per unit gained (0, *new*), Muster Rites (0, *changing*), The Funeral Games (0, *new*) |
| Engine | The Call to the Spear (I, *new*), The Open Muster (I, *new*), The Art of War (I, *changing*), Songs of the Fallen (I, *changing*) |
| Finishers | The Nation in Arms (IV), The Legions' Labour (IV, *deferred*) |
| Flex | Living off the Land (II, *new*), The Standing Army (0) |

**Other candidates:**
- Dominion's own: The King's Levy (0); "+30% production toward first- and second-age units" (0, *new*); The Armourers' Secret (0, *new*); War Foundries (I); Reserve Officers (III); Total War (III); The Grand Army (IV);
- in conversation: The Hall of the Slain (I, *proposed*) and The Regimental Rolls (I, *proposed*);
- shared: Rites of Passage (0, Devotion), The Sacred Host (I, Devotion), The Sword of State (I, Magisterial).

**Gives up:** unit quality and citizens. Feeding units to rival empires also costs happiness through weariness.
**Partners:** Devotion (faith into units and back) and Expansionist (its levies).
**Leaders:** Joan of Arc (kills pay faith), and Mithridates with the faster camps.

**What it needs**
- **A finisher that is in the game.** The Legions' Labour is the perfect one (the army builds the Opus) but it is deferred. Without it, the host's late game is "more units". An alternative is a card that cashes in the whole game's losses, such as "+1% science and culture for every unit lost this game, at most +25%" (a Valhalla card).
- **One job per card.** The user's "gaining a unit grants science equal to its strength" and The Art of War do the same job, so one must change.
- **Attention.** This build makes the most pieces. It works only because units are meant to be spent. Keep the free-unit cards making the best unit rather than many weak ones.

## 3. The steppe raiders

**The fantasy.** Scythians, Huns, Mongols, Vikings. Riders who strike anywhere and are gone before the defenders arrive, who grow rich on what others built, and who need no cities of anyone else's, only what is in them.

**Why it's fun**
- **The power fantasy:** speed and untouchability, and taking the fruits of other people's work.
- **The moments:**
  - a rider strikes, pillages and rides out of reach in one turn (The Swift Riders);
  - a caravan seized on the road (The Razzia);
  - the rival building the Opus watches its farms burn.
- **The decisions:** where to strike, and when to run. Raiders rarely lose units, so they rarely pay weariness. Their units live long enough to become Veterans and Elites, so ranks reward the style.
- **Moderate attention:** fast units need orders. Fewer, ranked riders keep it manageable.

**How it plays**
- **Age 1:** pastures and camps feed the herds, and barbarian camps are the first raids (The Cattle Raid, Blood Brothers).
- **Age 2:** horse lords strike and move on, ignoring zones of control, and pillage pays production and culture.
- **Age 3:** horse archers with range, armies living off the land, and caravans seized.
- **Age 4:** *nothing today* (see below).

**How it wins:** by stopping the empire building the Magnum Opus (raiding its land and caravans and starving its Opus town), while plunder pays for its own research and drafts.

**The eleven chairs**

| Part | Cards |
|---|---|
| Foundation | The Horse Herds (0, *changing*), The Cattle Raid (0, *new*), Blood Brothers (0) |
| Engine | Horse Lords (I), The Swift Riders (I, *new*), Riders of the Steppe (II), Spoils of War (II), The Razzia (I, *proposed*) |
| Finishers | *none in the game* |
| Flex | Living off the Land (II, *new*), Master Bowyers (II) |

**Other candidates:**
- Dominion's own: Clear the Frontier (0), The Pacified Marches (I), The Quartermasters (I), Total War (III), The Grand Coalition (IV), Circumvallation (IV, *deferred*);
- in conversation: Field Promotion (I, *proposed*);
- shared: The Barbarian Frontier (I, Expansionist).

**Gives up:** holding land and defence. It has the weakest peacetime floor.
**Partners:** Commerce (plunder turned into gold), and Expansionist (camps and pastures).
**Leader:** Modu Chanyu (mounted movement; pillage pays half again; pastures pay production and faith).

**What it needs**
- **Its whole late game.** Nothing in tiers III and IV pays off raiding. Some shapes to consider:
  - **Tribute:** an empire you have pillaged this war pays you each turn until peace.
  - **The great raid:** pillaging takes the improvement's build cost as production.
  - **Opus denial:** un-defer Circumvallation, or a raid that sets back a rival's Opus.
  - **A rank card:** Elite mounted units heal fully when they pillage.
- **A peacetime floor.** Barbarians carry it early. In the midgame, a raider at peace has little to do.

## 4. The conqueror

**The fantasy.** Alexander, Rome, Assyria. Every city taken makes the next one easier: the conquered pay for the next war, their scholars fill your libraries, and their great works grace your capital. The empire grows by the sword, and ends by seizing the Magnum Opus itself.

**Why it's fun**
- **The power fantasy:** the snowball. Captures are jackpots, and an empire visibly grows across the map.
- **The moments:**
  - the walls fall;
  - a capital changes hands;
  - Spolia takes a rival's half-built Opus;
  - The Captured Marvel pays off a wonder city.
- **The decisions:** what each conquest is for, and what each costs. Keep the town and garrison it through its unrest, or raze it. Win cleanly, or pay weariness for a grinding war. Push on while the veterans live, or make peace.
- **Investment, not tedium:** sieges are the engaging part of war, and holding a conquest is a few clear decisions.

**How it plays**
- **Age 1:** a general and an early edge in strength. A rush is possible but the build starts in age 2.
- **Age 2:** the first walls fall. Each capture pays (Triumphs, Take the City), and Hegemony speeds every town after one.
- **Age 3:** siege trains and Total War. Captured towns join the economy once pacified (Conquered Archives).
- **Age 4:** each captured town feeds the empire's science, culture and Opus (The Annexed Provinces, Imperium), and Spolia steals the leader's work.

**How it wins:** captured towns pay into its own Magnum Opus (Imperium), and taking the builder's town takes its progress (Spolia).

**The eleven chairs**

| Part | Cards |
|---|---|
| Foundation | Battle Honours (0, *changing*: a commission for a general), The Marshals (I), The Keys of the City (I, *proposed*) |
| Engine | Siege Works (II), Hegemony (II), Triumphs (II), Conquered Archives (II), The Siege Train (III) |
| Finishers | The Annexed Provinces (IV), Imperium or Spolia (IV) |
| Flex | The Proconsuls (IV) |

**Other candidates:**
- commissions: Take the City (II), The Grand Campaign (IV), Hold the Capitals (IV), The Captured Marvel (IV);
- Dominion's own: Total War (III), The Grand Coalition (IV), Master Bowyers (II);
- in conversation: The Sack (*proposed*), Captive Greece (*proposed*), Field Promotion (*proposed*);
- shared: Carried-Off Scholars (Scientific), Convert It or Tax It and The Crusader Orders (Devotion), Looted Art and The New Code (Magisterial).

**Gives up:** its own towns' development, and happiness to unrest and weariness. It needs targets: a peaceful neighbourhood leaves it idle.
**Partners:** Devotion (the holy war), Scientific (carried-off scholars), and Magisterial (looted art, a new code).
**Leader:** Mithridates (strength against wider empires).

**What it needs**
- **Its early half.** It is the most complete build from tier II on, but tiers 0 and I hold almost nothing that prepares a conquest. The Keys of the City and Field Promotion would give it a start.
- **Fewer cards that do the same job.** It has more capture payouts than it can seat (Triumphs, Carried-Off Scholars, Take the City, The Grand Campaign, The Captured Marvel, Looted Art). Several could merge.

## What the four builds show about the deck

- **Only the conqueror has a full arc to tier IV today.**
  - The garrison state has its finishers but no moment.
  - The host's best finisher is deferred.
  - The raiders have nothing after tier II.
  - The conqueror has more payouts than chairs.
- **Cards that belong to no build:**
  - The Soldiers' Purse, The War Hall Schools, Drill Manuals, The General Staff, Armoury Guilds and Arsenals;
  - Marine Drill and Naval Supremacy;
  - The Camp Chaplains, Blooded Spears and The Bronze Smiths.

  Most of these are flat riders on buildings that saturate at one per town. They are the "individually useful" cards the user named: each pays the same in any Dominion seat, so they compete for chairs without building anything. Candidates to rework into build cards, or cut. Naval play has no build at all; that is fine if it stays a minor thread.
- **Bridges, which serve two or more builds and make pivots possible:**
  - Living off the Land (host, raiders, conqueror);
  - The Standing Army (garrison state, host);
  - The Proconsuls (conqueror, garrison state: a captured town held by a garrison);
  - Total War (host, raiders, conqueror);
  - Songs of the Fallen (host, conqueror);
  - Blood Brothers (raiders, host);
  - The Marshalate (garrison state, conqueror).
- **The war costs pull the builds apart,** which is what makes them feel different:
  - the garrison state makes others pay weariness;
  - the host pays it unless it feeds barbarians;
  - the raiders avoid it and earn ranks;
  - the conqueror must manage both weariness and unrest.
