What the player sees

The charter is dealt like any Order. Its face keeps the A3 frame: the suit for the deck, the ink for the type, the rank for the rarity. The chip says it takes no chair.

Two things are new. The first clause says what it opens and that no other empire may research it. Under the clauses sits a slip: a small piece of the star chart with the technology, its age, its price as a list, and the turns at the current rate.

Why

The choice here is a chair card now, or a research path. The player cannot judge the path without its price, and the price is not an ordinary column price. So the price list goes where the choice is made.

The slip uses the star chart's night colours. The player learns at once that this card is about the chart.

Extends

  • offerCard.ts faceBody: one optional slip block, beside the commission counter.
  • statecraft/describers.ts: a live clause replaces today's struck-through "drafting this opens the study of mechanics for research".
  • deckBadges.ts (the charter chip) and suitFace.ts: unchanged.

What the player sees

The card takes a rubber stamp: Opened · Mechanics. The sheet closes. One toast says "Mechanics is open for research. Only you may research it."

A click on the toast opens the star chart, centred on Mechanics, with a ring around it for a moment. Research does not change. Horology goes on.

Why

The pick must not choose research for the player. The toast is the one line of news, and its click is the shortest way to the node. The game already makes "Granary completed in Uruk" a toast you can click.

The stamp is the "thunk" face that occasion cards already use. A charter pays nothing per turn, so a count-up figure would be wrong. A stamp is right.

Extends

  • cardStamp.ts: the thunk face, with "Opened" and the technology's name.
  • notifications.ts: one new NotificationAction kind that opens the star chart at a technology. toasts.ts needs no change.
  • techTree.ts centreOn, and a one-time ring class.

The hover card. In the game it follows the pointer; here it is docked.

Try: hover Mechanics. Click it to aim, or shift-click to queue it after Horology. Then press Narrow window below and scroll the chart.

Prototype view, not a game control:

What the player sees

A shelf at the foot of the chart, under a sepia rule: Charters · yours alone. It holds only the charter technologies Hypatia has drafted: Casting, Cataloguing and Mechanics. Each sits one column right of its ordinary parent, with a dotted sepia line from the parent. The age washes run through the shelf.

The node face shows the total price, as every node does. The hover card lists the price: the column, the charter premium, The Commonplace Book's discount, the total.

A military charter gives a trait to a whole line of units you already build. On the face, a trait shows its name, its bonus and its ability where an unlock shows a price, because a trait changes no price.

Why a shelf, and when

The lanes are the user's drawing. A shelf leaves the sky as it is, keeps every charter path in one row, and keeps "a column is a price": column 8 times one and a half.

The shelf exists only after the first charter is drafted. Before that the chart has no shelf, no rule and no tab. The draft that brings the first charter also brings the shelf (the two crops above).

Considered and set aside: a node beside its parent in the sky (moves the lanes), and a separate charter screen (a new screen).

The tab, when the shelf is out of view

If no charter node is in view, a sepia tab sits on the chart's edge, on the side where the nearest one is: Charters, the count, and an arrow. A click scrolls to that node and rings it, as the toast does. When a charter node comes into view, the tab goes. Press Narrow window and scroll to try it.

Extends

  • techTree.ts: renderChart, paintNode, techCard, and the tab, which reads the stage's scroll position and moves it as centreOn does.
  • techFit.ts: packChart packs the shelf as a second field; fitPacked shrinks both together.
  • tech.ts: a cost list explainTechCost and its fold (rule 5).
  • style.css: .tech-node.is-charter, the tab, and one token for sepia on night.

What the player sees

The technology splash, as for any technology, with one new eyebrow line: "your charter technology". Under it, the tally says what changed: 3 catapults gained Onager (+2, +1 range), and 2 composite bowmen gained Repeating Crossbow.

On the board each piece glows once. Its type does not change, so it keeps its name, health and rank. The unit card shows the trait after the name, Catapult · Onager, and lists strength and range with the trait as its own line. On turn 120 Natural Philosophy turns it into a Trebuchet in place, and it is still Trebuchet · Onager, at range 3.

A toast says where the pieces are. A click pans the camera there. On the chart, Mechanics is now known, and it wears the gilt corner of the lead.

Why

A trait is not a new unit, so there is no new row, no flip and no new price. The trait tally takes the upgrade tally's place in the splash, because both answer one question: what did this technology do to my army? The trait belongs to the line, so every later upgrade keeps it (ruled 2026-10-21: "It keeps it once it upgrades").

The glow marks the pieces that changed this turn. A lasting badge on the piece is a later art decision, so none is drawn.

Extends

  • turnSplash.ts announceTech: one optional eyebrow.
  • tech.ts: a trait tally beside describeUpgrade ("3 catapults gained Onager").
  • unitPanel.ts: the trait after the unit's name, and its lines in the strength and range lists. The piece fingerprint does not change.
  • toasts.ts: a toast with a pan action, the kind that exists.

Why

The board is public and the rules are public. Your draft and your research are not.

A unit's figures are lists in this game (rule 5), so the trait is one labelled line, as terrain or a general is in a fight. The line prints on every unit of the line for every viewer. It says nothing about Hypatia that the unit on the board does not already say.

Where the lead does not appear
  • Not on the top bar. It changes a few times a game.
  • Not in the empire drawer. That shows what you are learning, not what you know.
  • Never on a rival's screen.

What the player sees

One figure in the star chart's head: lead 4. Its hover card lists the four technologies and the empires counted against. Each node in the lead wears a gilt corner. A seated card that reads the lead, such as The Secret Arts, shows it in its stamp.

Why here

The lead is a fact about research, so it lives on the research screen. Charter technologies are always in it, which makes "yours alone" a number the player can watch. Mathematics left the lead on turn 90, when Taizong learned it. The corner shows that too.

It counts met empires only, so no hidden information leaks.

Extends

  • techTree.ts: the head figure, and one class in paintNode.
  • Feature B's memo over met empires, in the sim.
  • cardStamp.ts: unchanged. The Secret Arts and The Proving Ground stamp what they pay.

Decisions, and the questions for you

Decisions

  1. A charter shelf at the foot of the star chart. Same columns and ages as the sky. Each node sits one column right of its one ordinary parent, so its column still sets its price. The user's lanes do not move.
  2. The shelf holds drafted charter technologies only, and does not exist before the first charter. Ruled 2026-10-21: "only show if drafted. Also, if no charters have been drafted, don't show the section."
  3. A tab shows a shelf that is out of view. It sits on the chart's edge toward the nearest charter node, with the count and an arrow. A click scrolls there and rings the node. Asked for in the same ruling: "some indicator that it's there for screens that aren't wide enough."
  4. The premium is a labelled line, and The Commonplace Book takes 15% off the whole charter price. The price list is on the hover card and the card's slip; a node face shows only the total. The list appears on any node with more than one price line. The discount ruled "great", 2026-10-21.
  5. A military charter gives a trait to a whole unit line, never a unit early. A small bonus (+1 at Tier 0, +2 at Tier II) and one ability. The line gains it on the turn the technology lands and keeps it through every upgrade: Catapult · Onager becomes Trebuchet · Onager. No new unit row, no change in cost. A node face shows the trait where an unlock shows a price. Ruled 2026-10-21: "It keeps it once it upgrades." This answers the earlier question about the line's next unit.
  6. The charter card carries a slip of the star chart. The technology, its age, its price list and its turns, on the card face.
  7. Drafting changes no research. A stamp, one toast, and a click to the chart.
  8. Research is unchanged. Click aims, shift-click queues. The plan strip, drawer and node add a small seal.
  9. Completion uses the technology splash. One eyebrow line, and a trait tally in the upgrade tally's place.
  10. Rivals see the board and the rules. Never your shelf, plan, progress or lead. A unit's hover card shows its trait after its name and as labelled lines, on every copy.
  11. Charter technologies do not count in a rival's gap. Answered "yes, this seems fun" on that recommendation. Read as agreeing, to confirm.
  12. The lead is one figure in the chart's head, a gilt corner on each node in it, and the stamp of any card that reads it. Nowhere else.

Added to existing pieces (no new inputs)

  • A NotificationAction kind that opens the star chart at a technology.
  • The out-of-view tab on the star chart. Its click only scrolls the chart, as a drag or the arrow keys already do.
  • The tech hover card: a price list, the line traits, and "these gain the trait" lines.
  • The unit card and the enemy-unit hover card: the trait after the unit's name, and its labelled lines.
  • The splash: a trait tally beside the upgrade tally.
  • One colour token: the charter sepia, lifted for the night chart.

Open questions

  1. Does a charter technology count toward an empire's age? Working assumption: no. Gunfounding (Æra IV) learned early would otherwise move Hypatia's age, the world's age and the beads.
  2. The parents, columns and glyphs of the charter technologies are placeholders. Your chart places them. I chose parents that keep each one inside its age.
