The rule: the number is always the faith you have banked. A second number shows only while the next god has a price. Everything else is in the tooltip and in the open card's Faith row. The figures below are samples.

  • Why not the prophet's cost as the second number. The slash would change its meaning without a sign, from "the next god" to "a unit you may not want". A count of rites would be a third meaning.

How the hover works

  • A short wait. The card grows 120 ms after the pointer arrives, so a pointer that only crosses the corner changes nothing. It grows in 120 ms, on height only.
  • The chips are pushed, not covered. That was the fault of the old peek. The card and the chips are one hover area, so the card stays grown while you move down to a chip, and a chip never jumps from under the pointer.
  • No growth while a mouse button is held, while a unit path is being drawn, or while a sheet is open. Keyboard focus inside the card grows it too.

Is it hard to make fast?

  • No. The growth is cheap. It is one CSS rule on one element: a hover selector, a height of 64 px, a 120 ms transition and a 120 ms delay. Nothing is computed on hover, because the two lines are always in the page and always current. The 3D map is not redrawn.
  • Two small parts are not CSS. The chips sit at a fixed place today, so they must move into the card's column or follow the card's height. And "no growth while a button is held" needs one class set by controls.ts.
  • The real cost is not speed. The routes and faith figures are hidden at rest, so you must move the pointer to read them, and a touch screen has no hover: there they show only in the open card. A red dot on the banner still tells you that a god waits.

Recommendation

Build Option A, revised. It keeps both figures in sight, it makes six clear buttons, and nothing in it depends on hover.

Option C, revised, is the smaller card, but it hides the two figures you asked to keep live, and it brings back a card that moves under the pointer.

Choices still open

  • In place of the row of keys: (a) names under the marks while open, or (b) four ordinary rows (drawn in the main mockup).
  • The faith states: one number always, a second only while the next god has a price, rose when it is met, a dot only when a god waits. Say if an affordable prophet should show a sign after all.
  • Beads: on the left with renown (drawn), or on the right beside the turn.
  • Routes: whether a free slot colours the figure, and whether an idle caravan should show a dot.

What is awkward

  • Four different paddings. The top part has 8, 10, 9 and 12 px on its four sides. The button row has none. No two edges of the card match.
  • Two alignment systems in one card. The text above starts 42 px from the left, after the banner. The four buttons below are centred in 62 px cells. Nothing in the bottom row lines up with anything in the top part.
  • The labels do not fit their cells. "Diplomacy" and "Standings" at 11 px leave about 5 px of air on each side, so the words almost touch the rules. "Routes" and "Faith" float in the same cells. The row looks uneven.
  • The heads are mixed. Two buttons have a mark and a figure, two have a mark alone. The four marks sit at four different offsets in their cells. "73/72" drops to 10.5 px beside "8/8" at 12 px.
  • Too many sizes and gaps. Six type sizes in 118 px of height: 13.5, 13, 12, 11.5, 11 and 10.5. The vertical gaps are 4, 2 and 3 px. There is no common step.
  • The arrow costs a column. It takes 30 px at the right of the top part. The bars stop short there, but the button row runs to the edge.
  • Three screens have two doors. Diplomacy, Trade and Standings each have a tab in the bar and a button in the corner. Statecraft has the Culture row and a row in the game menu. The routes figure 8/8 prints twice on one screen.
  • Two doors mislead. The faith figure in the bar opens the Ledger and not the Faith sheet. Standings has two doors and no key.
