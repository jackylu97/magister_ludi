Order card faces

This round is about the card's face only. Round four's screen stands. Each face below puts the effect inside an arcane card's composition, in the way an old printed card or emblem carries its words. Nothing here is built.

Four faces follow, each from a different tradition. Each is drawn at 2x for five real cards: a short effect, a long one whose condition is not met, a commission with a counter, a rule card with no number, and a Doctrine. Then at true size: the hand card, the three rarities, a sealed card, an unmet condition, and a card played but not confirmed. The card texts are live rows, word for word. The live figures are samples.

What every face shares

  • The device. The deck's suit, in the game's own path data, made into an engraved device: a hatched disc, a double ring, a glory of rays, and the suit with a cut shadow. It is drawn in the type's ink. A card that pays nothing now loses its rays.
  • Rubrication. The figures and the yield words are in rubric red, as a scribe marks what matters. The sentence is never shortened. A named thing is in small capitals.
  • Rarity is the richness of the border. Common: a plain double rule. Uncommon: a hatched band and corner flourishes. Rare: a gilt band with a punched pattern, a gilt ground behind the device, a black tier plate, sun and moon or stars in the corners. A Doctrine has the heaviest gilt frame.
  • The tier is a Roman numeral on a plate at the head. A seal is a red cord and wax with the turns left. A staged card has a dashed edge and faint art until Confirm.
  • Size. Each face is drawn at 210 by 258. That is taller than round four's 210 by 194. See the note on the table at the end.

  • They are used on Order and Doctrine cards only. The rest of the interface keeps its faces.

Face 1The emblem

From the emblem books (Alciato, Maier). Three parts: the motto above, the picture, and beneath it the subscription that explains it. The card's name is the motto. The effect is the subscription, opened by a red initial. The live line is the colophon at the foot.

At 2x (420 by 516).

At true size: the hand card, then the played card and its states.

  • The words in the art. The effect reads as the verse under an emblem. The first figure is lifted into a small tablet in the corner of the picture (+15 with the gold mark), and the full sentence stays beneath. A counter is beads on a wire in the colophon.
  • Hand and played. One face. A fanned hand shows the top of each card, which is the motto and the picture.
  • Smallest text at 210 by 258: 12 px (the colophon). The effect is 13 px on up to five lines. A name over 21 letters drops to 11.4 px.
  • Drawing work. Once: the frame, the picture field, the rays. For each suit: nothing new, the game's path. For each rarity: a border band and corner marks (three). From the card's data: name, tier, text, initial, live line. No art for any single card.

Face 2The banderole

From the tarot and from speech scrolls in prints. The device fills an engraved field. The name is on a title cartouche at the foot, as on a tarot trump. A short effect runs on a ribbon that folds across the card. A long one hangs as a small charter.

At 2x (420 by 516).

At true size: the hand card, then the played card and its states.

  • The words in the art. Up to three folds of ribbon, about 27 letters each. A longer effect becomes a hanging charter with a cut lower edge and a red initial. The first figure sits in a coin at the head, as a playing card shows its rank. The live line is the second line of the cartouche.
  • Hand and played. One face. The hand card is the device over the cartouche.
  • Smallest text at 210 by 258: 11.5 px (the live line). The ribbon is 12.8 px, the charter 12.5 px.
  • Risk. The ribbon breaks a sentence where the fold falls, so lines break by length, not by sense. About two cards in five are long enough to need the charter, so the deck shows two forms.
  • Drawing work. Once: the field, the ribbon fold and its curl, the charter, the cartouche, the coin. From the card's data: the same as Face 1, and the line breaks.

Face 3The roundel

From alchemical and astrological plates. A round plate with the card's name running round its rim and the live line on the lower rim. The four corners hold signs: the value, the chair type, the tier, and a star, moon or sun for the rarity. The effect is cut on a tablet beneath.

At 2x (420 by 516).

At true size: the hand card, then the played card and its states.

  • The words in the art. The name and the live line follow the circle. The value is set large in the corner (+15 over the gold mark; 3/4 for a counter). The whole sentence is on the tablet, centred, as an inscription.
  • Hand and played. One face. The hand card is the device and the name.
  • Smallest text at 210 by 258: 11 px (the name on the rim). The tablet is 12.5 px on up to five lines.
  • Risk. Text on a curve is the hardest to read, and the rim holds only about 26 letters, which is the longest name in the sample. Centred text suits short effects better than long ones.
  • Drawing work. Once: the plate with its rings and two text paths, the tablet. For each rarity: the ground of the rim and the corner sign. From the card's data: name, tier, type, value, text, live line.

Face 4The rubric leaf

From manuscript and early printed pages. The card is a ruled leaf. The device is a framed miniature. The name is the rubric, in red. The effect opens with a large red initial. The live line is a gloss in the margin, pointed at by a small hand, which the game already uses as its manicule.

At 2x (420 by 516).

At true size: the hand card, then the played card and its states.

  • The words in the art. Here the words are the art: red and black ink, the initial, the ruled lines, old-style figures. It gives the text the most room of the four.
  • Hand and played. One face. The hand card is the miniature over the red name.
  • Smallest text at 210 by 258: 11 px (the deck and type line). The effect is 13 px, the gloss 12 px, the name 17 px.
  • It also works wide. The miniature and title sit beside the text, so this face can be laid out at round four's 210 by 194. I did not draw that form.
  • Risk. It is the least like a playing card: a page, not a trump. Old-style figures are a little less plain than lining ones.
  • Drawing work. Once: the leaf, the miniature frame, the initial's style, the pointing hand. From the card's data: everything else.

The tableFace 1 on the cloth

Six played cards in a row, at 1440 wide, true size: two Military chairs and four Economic. Chairs 2 and 5 are sealed, chair 5 pays nothing now, and chair 6 was played this visit.

  • What the taller card costs. At 1440 by 900, two rows of 210 by 258 take 528 px. They fit with the register in its compact form (about 100 px) and the hand as a rail of card tops. So the Doctrines' effect text drops to three lines, and the hand needs H.
  • Smaller windows. The faces are drawn for 1440 by 900 and up. At 1280 by 720 and 1024 by 768 the text would fall under 11 px if the card only shrank. There the table needs either Face 4 in its wide form or a table that scrolls. That is a real limit of an artful face.

My pick

Face 1, the emblem. It is the most like an arcane card and the easiest to read: a picture with its verse beneath, which is what an Order is.

Take the coin from Face 2 if you want the number larger, and keep Face 4's wide form for small windows.

What to choose

  • The face: 1, 2, 3 or 4, or parts of them.
  • The text faces: EB Garamond and IM Fell English on the cards, or the game's present faces.
  • Rubric red for figures and yields, or the yields in their own inks.
  • The taller card (210 by 258) with a compact register and a hand rail at 1440 by 900, or a shorter face.
  • Small windows: a wide form of the face, or a scrolling table.

The chair marks and the corner flourishes are my drawings. The dagger marks the reason line, which is a new reading.
