Diplomacy screen: Layout B on smaller screens

Second round. You chose Layout B and asked if it is too wide. These frames show one responsive system at five window sizes, and a narrower sheet at 1440. Drawings only. Each frame is the whole game window at true size.

The rules that make it fit

  • Sheet size. Width = the window less 56 px, up to a maximum. Height = the window less 90 px, up to 860 px. The sheet is centred and never stretches past the maximum.
  • Sheet 1300 px or wider (only with the 1340 maximum): rail 226 px, column 264 px, full loan notes ("lent by the French, 9 turns").
  • Sheet 1100 to 1299 px: rail 196 px, column 236 px, paper 216 px. The loan note takes its short form: "loan 9t · French", "loan 12t · yours", "loan 18t · theirs". The turns come first, so a long name is cut and the turns are not. The full sentence is on hover.
  • Sheet under 1100 px (a window under 1156 px): the rail becomes a 56 px strip of shields, and every court is still one click away. The name shows on hover, and the selected court's name is in the line above the desk. The right column folds into that line (name, standing, a "Standing and deals" button, the waiting offer, Declare war). The button opens the same column as a drawer over the right edge. The trays get the freed width back, so the full loan notes return.
  • Sheet under 720 px high (a window under 810 px high): rows are 22 px (24 px above), a rail row is 40 px (46 px above), the head is 56 px (80 px above), and the "not shown" line moves into the Luxuries label.
  • What never changes: the paper is in the middle with the propose control at its foot, the two trays are on its sides, gold and rights are above the luxuries. If a side has more luxuries than fit, only the luxury list scrolls inside its tray.

Choices left for you

  • The maximum width: 1180 px (recommended, short loan notes) or 1340 px (the first round, full loan notes on large screens only). A value between them changes little: the full notes need a tray of about 225 px, which needs a sheet of 1300 px.
  • The short loan note. "loan 9t · French" is drawn. Another form is the turns only ("9t") with the rest on hover.
  • The narrow window. The drawer is drawn. The other option is a second act tab ("Standing") in place of the drawer. The drawer keeps the deal in view. The tab does not.
  • Shields at 1024. The rail shows shields only. If names matter more than tray width there, the rail can stay at 196 px and the trays use the short notes.
  • Still open from the first round: the title ("Foreign courts" or "Diplomacy"), their last word, where Declare war goes, and the order of the groups.

How it differs from the rest of the game

  • Colour. The screen has its own palette of grey-greens written as hex values (#f4f0e3 ground, #405846 green button, #e1e7d3 green selection, #e9ebdf green panel). The Ledger and the Reliquary use the SS1 tokens: parchment, ink, and cinnabar for the one main button.
  • Type. Six sizes of the display serif stack in one view (38, 32, 26, 25, 22 and 21 px) for the title, the court, the quote, the section, the side name and the table lines. Labels and notes are 8 to 11 px. The Ledger uses one large title, Fraunces 17 px headings, and 13 px rows.
  • Rules and cards. The Ledger separates rows with a 1 px rule, puts an ink rule under a column head and a double rule over a total. Diplomacy puts each luxury in its own rounded card (4 px radius, 67 px high with its note), inside a column, inside a rounded panel.
  • Buttons. Green fill, 10 px text, 4 px radius, stacked at full width. The rest of the game uses .btn: parchment, a 1 px edge, a 3 px radius, and cinnabar with an ink border for the main act.
  • The court and its quote. The selected court is shown twice (the roster row and a 104 px heading with a 45 × 56 px shield). The envoy's sentence is a 26 px quote with a 2 px coloured stripe on its left, the only such stripe in the game, and it takes 90 px.
  • Spacing. Six bands stack with 16 px between them and 22 to 26 px of padding. A roster row is about 100 px. A Ledger row is 28 px.
  • Structure. The deals in force are on a second tab (Agreements). The "On the table" panel and the propose button are a normal column, so they leave the view when the player scrolls the luxuries. The roster foot holds a paragraph of rules.
  • Numbers. Counts are 15 px mono with an 8 px label below ("1 to offer"). The Ledger puts figures in one right-aligned column at the row's size.

It was drawn with placeholder fonts before the Ledger and the Reliquary were built, so it does not use their ruled tables or their three parts.

What the screen shows and does today

  • Roster: each court you have met, with its shield, leader, people, relation (at peace, at war since a turn, truce with turns left), a note ("A bargain is running.", "They have offered peace.") and a count of running deals.
  • Roster foot: your luxury kinds and copies, and the rule that only luxuries are traded.
  • Court heading: shield, leader, people, relation, and war weariness when you have losses to them.
  • Envoy: one sentence that changes with the state (greeting, considering, accepted, declined, a counter-offer).
  • Each side of the table: gold in hand, luxuries with copies to offer, "Last copy", the loan note with the lender and turns left, open borders (greyed with the reason), gold once, gold a turn, and towns in a war.
  • On the table: what you give and what you receive, as lines.
  • Agreements tab: offers waiting (theirs and yours), the peace paper in a war, and each deal in force with its turns left.
  • Choose a court.
  • Declare war (with a confirm step). In a war: offer peace, withdraw the offer, accept peace, send their envoy home.
  • Tick a luxury on either side (one copy a deal). Tick open borders on either side. Type gold once and gold a turn on either side. In a war, tick towns on either side.
  • Propose the exchange. The court answers at once.
  • Ask "What would make this work?" and "What would you give for this?". Clear the table.
  • Accept or decline an offer from them. Withdraw an offer of yours.
  • A deal can hold five kinds of item (DealTerms): gold once, gold a turn, luxuries, open borders, and towns (peace only). A deal runs 20 turns (dealTurns).

The worst case that every layout below is drawn for

  • 11 courts. A game has at most 12 seats (maxPlayers in data/rules.json), so a player meets at most 11.
  • 14 luxuries on your side, 8 on theirs, 2 not shown. The game has 24 luxuries. A luxury that both own is hidden, so the two sides together show at most 24 rows. 14 and 8 with 2 hidden is such a case.
  • Five greyed rows (Gems, Salt, Dyes, Wine, Coral): the receiver holds the luxury only on loan. The note names the lender and the turns left. After the ruling "A borrowed luxury cannot be lent on", the giver's side never lists a borrowed copy, so the drawings list none.
  • Every kind of item: gold once, gold a turn, open borders, luxuries, and towns. Towns show only in a war, so the worst case shows the Towns group closed, and the third Layout B frame shows it open.
  • 3 deals in force with the selected court, an offer waiting from them, a refused proposal (their last word), two wars and one truce in the roster, and offers waiting from two other courts.
  • A war ends every deal between the two empires. So one court never shows a war and a list of deals in force together. The drawings show the two as separate states.

What all three layouts share

  • Tokens from SS1 only: parchment, ink, the 1 px rule, cinnabar for the one main button, Newsreader for the title and names, Fraunces for headings and small-caps labels, Instrument Sans for rows, IBM Plex Mono for figures.
  • Ruled rows, not cards. An ink rule under each column head, a 1 px rule between rows, a double rule over the total. This is the Ledger's table.
  • The luxury states. On the table: a filled box and a tinted row. Greyed: a dashed rule or border, grey text, and an italic note with the lender and turns (still pressable, as in SS4). Hidden: one italic line that names the luxuries both own.
  • No tabs for a court's facts. The deals in force and the waiting offers are in view with the table.
  • Their last word is one italic line beside the propose control. The game has no attitude score and no price for a deal, so no layout draws one.
  • Room for more acts. A row of act tabs chooses the paper (Exchange, Peace terms). Dashed tabs mark where a new act goes. A new kind of item is a new group of rows.
  • Spacing scale: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32 px. Item row 24 px. Courts row 27 px (table) or 46 px (rail). Button 28 px. Sheet padding 24 to 36 px. The sheet in A and B is 1340 × 810 px, the size of the Ledger.

Gains

  • It is the Ledger's own structure, so it reads as the same game at once.
  • Both sides are aligned row by row with full notes on one line. 14 luxuries fit at 24 px a row with no inner scroll.
  • The courts table has columns, so 11 courts compare well (standing, deals, offers).

Costs

  • The right page is full in the worst case (about 15 px spare). A sixth kind of item or a list of towns needs an inner scroll in the two columns. The foot stays in view.
  • The courts table takes half the sheet when you have met one court.
  • The total is two lines of text. It is less clear than a document that the deal is one paper.

Build work: medium

  • New two-page shell (the Ledger's .ldg-book and page styles can be shared).
  • The roster becomes a table. The item rows become ruled rows. The Agreements tab is removed.
  • The foot is pinned. No change to the rules or commands.

Spacing

  • Page padding 22 to 36 px. Courts row 27 px. Item row 24 px. Group label 22 px. Column gap 28 px.
  • Foot: double rule, 8 px gaps, buttons 28 px.

  • Where offers show. Three places: the notice beside the turn button (an envelope, a count in cinnabar, "offers waiting"), an envelope on that court's row in the rail, and a "Waiting for your answer" row in the right column of the selected court.
  • The notice, open. A ruled list on parchment with one row a court: shield, leader, and one line of terms. A press on a row opens the screen with that court selected. With one offer, a press on the notice opens it at once, as today. The notice never blocks the turn, as today.
  • How an offer opens. Their terms are loaded on the paper, with the title "as the Abbasids wrote it". The buttons become Accept and Decline for an exchange, or "Accept peace" and "Send them home" for a peace. "Change the terms" keeps the terms on the paper for you to edit and send as your own proposal. The rules have no counter-offer, so this is the same act as today.
  • The frame above shows the peace case: the act tab is Peace terms, the Towns group is open in both trays, the column gives the war and the weariness, and the roster marks the other war.

Gains

  • It is the Reliquary's structure, and it is closest to the present three columns, so the player's habits stay.
  • The paper is the deal. It reads as one sentence list, stays in view at full height, and holds their last word and the propose control. An incoming offer opens as the same paper with other buttons.
  • The right column is a list that can grow: standing, deals, offers, other acts. Paper acts are tabs on the desk.

Costs

  • The trays are about 225 px wide. A loan note must be short ("lent by the French, 9 turns").
  • Three parts need the full 1340 px sheet. At 1024 px the right column must go under the rail or become a drawer.
  • A tray holds 14 luxuries and about 3 towns. More than that needs an inner scroll in the tray. The paper and the propose control stay in view.

Build work: medium

  • The roster column stays and gets rail rows. The middle column becomes the paper and moves between the sides. A new right column takes the Agreements tab's content.
  • The item rows become ruled rows. The envoy band and the court heading are removed.
  • The deal notice gets new styles and one line of terms a row. No change to the rules or commands.

Spacing

  • Head 80 px. Body padding 16 to 24 px. Gaps between parts 20 px. Rail row 46 px with 5 px between.
  • Tray row 24 px. Paper clause row 26 px. Desk padding 14 px. Tray to paper gap 16 px.

Gains

  • It is the most compact: 14 luxuries take four rows of chips, and about 120 px stay free in the worst case. The sheet is 630 px high, so the map and the turn button stay in view.
  • One court's facts are two lines. Nothing is repeated.
  • Gold, borders and towns are one row of controls, so a new kind of item is one more control in that row.

Costs

  • Chips look less like the Ledger's ruled tables than rows do. The notes are short ("last"), and a greyed chip is wide.
  • The tabs show 11 courts by short name with a one-word standing. A longer fact (a truce's turns, who offered peace) needs a hover or the strip.
  • Deals in force are chips in one line. More than three or four need a second line or a count. A list of 8 towns a side needs a second chip block.

Build work: medium

  • New small shell. The roster becomes tabs. The rows become chips. The Agreements tab becomes the strip.
  • The foot is pinned. No change to the rules or commands.
  • More test work than A or B: the chip states (on, greyed, disabled) replace every row test in dealPanel.test.ts that reads the row markup.

Spacing

  • Title 42 px. Court tabs 46 px. Standing and deals 76 px. Act tabs 38 px. Padding 24 px at the sides.
  • Chip 26 px high with 6 px gaps. Control row 26 px. Foot about 110 px with 28 px buttons.

Recommendation

Layout B, the treaty desk. It keeps the whole deal, the standing and every court in view in the worst case, and the paper gives incoming offers and future acts one clear place. Use A's courts table only if you want to compare courts by column. Use C if you prefer a small sheet over the map to a full sheet.

Choices left for you

  • The layout: A, B or C. Parts can mix: for example B with C's chips in the trays, or B with A's courts table in place of the rail.
  • Rows or chips for luxuries. Rows keep the full notes and match the Ledger. Chips are denser.
  • The title. The drawings keep "Foreign courts" in the Ledger's title style. The corner panel names the door "Diplomacy". Choose one name.
  • Their last word. The drawings keep it as one italic line. It can be removed, or it can stay only after an answer.
  • Declare war. In B it is in "Other acts" at the foot of the right column, away from the propose button. It can go beside the court's name.
  • The 1024 px window. B needs a rule for a narrow window: the right column under the rail, or behind a button. Not drawn here.
  • The order of the groups. The drawings put gold and rights above luxuries, so that the fixed controls never move when the luxury list grows. Today luxuries are first.
