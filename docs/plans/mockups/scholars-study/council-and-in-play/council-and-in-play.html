The totals, made slight

N3 and N4Two treatments, at real size

Both set the totals as one fine line of eight figures: the six yields, a hairline, then happiness and authority. The figures are 23 px in the card typeface, in the yield colours, each with a 15 px mark. There are no boxes and no block. N3 puts the line under the head and rules it off with a hairline. N4 puts it in the head itself, at the right. I pick N4. It costs no height, the eye finds it where the top bar of the map screen has its figures, and the chips for a pending change sit beside each figure, as the third drawing shows. N3 is the choice if the head must also hold other things later.

The In play screen

Everything in force, tucked. The government column is where it is on the Council screen. The council is its own stack of edges, one for each chair. I chose a stack and not small cards for two reasons. It is the same object as every other stack on this screen, so there is one thing to learn. And eight edges take less room than eight small cards and still show each name and figure. Nothing moves here.

The rule for a stack with many pieces.
An edge is never squeezed. A stack grows downward, one full edge for each piece, up to the top piece and eight edges. Past that it ends in one "more" edge of the same size, and a click expands the whole stack in the tray. Thirteen legacies are drawn here: the top piece, eight edges, and "4 more".

Eight is the number that fits above the tray at 900 px of height.

The door, and the arrival

In play has three doors. From the map: a fifth button in the row at the foot of the corner panel, with the count. From the Council screen: the link in the head. From anywhere: the I key. I did not add a third line to the corner panel, because its two lines are the two things the player waits for.

D1, A1 to A3The door on the map, and a wonder that arrives

A1: the lapis plate presents the wonder in its own form. A2: on Continue it flies to the In play button in the corner panel, the count goes from 9 to 10, and a dot appears. A3: the next time In play opens, the wonder is on top of its stack with a glow, and the dot on the door is gone.

Cost, risks, cut order

What the rules already give. The game already measures a figure for each turn for an Order, a Doctrine, the government, a belief and a legacy (explainCardImpact in src/sim/cardImpact.ts). Its list of subjects has no wonder and no timed effect yet. So only those two kinds need new work in the rules. The same measure already reports happiness and authority, as its meter lines, so those figures need no new work for the kinds it covers.

Risks

  • A card with no plate may look unfinished. Blood Brothers ends with empty paper. That is the point of your comment, but look at C8 and C11 to see if the empty foot is acceptable.
  • Garamond figures are not tabular by default. The plate uses lining figures. In a row of plates the digits do not line up from card to card, which does not matter on separate cards. The totals in the head now use the same typeface, so they are not tabular either. They are eight separate figures, so nothing has to line up.
  • Three yields on the narrow card. At 176 px, three figures at 17 px fill the plate. A fourth would need two lines.
  • A deep stack. A stack shows its top piece and eight edges, then a "more" edge (I5). A full stack is about 510 px tall, so the open tray covers its foot.
  • Happiness and food share a colour, and authority and production share one. These are the game's own colours for the two meters. Only the mark tells them apart, so the mark must stay beside every figure.
  • Eight figures and four chips fill about 800 px of the head at 1440 wide. At 1180 wide the line would need N3, its own row.
  • The totals are slight now. They are 23 px, not 30 px, and they share the head with the title. If they are too easy to miss in play, N3 gives them their own line.
  • The door is small. A fifth button in the corner panel is 60 px wide. If players do not find it, the Council link and the I key remain.
  • Eleven chairs. 176 px is the limit at 1440 wide. A narrower window needs a scroll in the council or a third row.

Cut order, if it must be smaller

  • First: the pending-change chips. The totals then change only after the drop.
  • Second: the arrival flight. The wonder is then on its stack with the glow, with no flight.
  • Third: figures for wonders and timed effects. Their edges show names only until the rules can measure them.
  • Keep: the pay plate, the line of totals, Confirm at the bottom right, and the tucked stacks.
