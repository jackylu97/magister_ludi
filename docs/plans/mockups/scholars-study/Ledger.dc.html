Under each turn’s total the curves shade statecraft, then great people, then religion, stacked up from the foot. The dashed tick is the turn an age opened. Each bar is the whole of what came in this turn, and upkeep is named beside it.

The book

The sheet opens as a book with two pages. The red money rules and the double rule come from an account book.

Each figure sits in a tabular column. A nil is a faint dash. The graphs are plates in the same book.

Left page: this turn

The rows are the eight sources, in the order of the game. The columns are the six yields.

Upkeep is a debit: red ink and a true minus sign, in the gold column. Each source keeps its ink in a small square, so the colours stay easy to see.

Right page: one yield

The chip that opened the sheet chooses the yield. Here it is production. The tabs at the edge open the other yields.

The curve of the open yield sits over its last eight turns. Each row is one turn, with the three sources that you chose: statecraft, great people and religion.

The fold-out plate

The plate holds the graphs of the Ledger of today: one bar a yield, split by the eight sources, and one curve a yield, with statecraft, great people and religion shaded under it.

The key to the eight inks prints once, at the foot. The bars agree with the left page, and each curve ends at the figure of this turn.

Your cards

Your cards is the part of this turn that the deck made, in the ink of statecraft. Confirm counts up the same figure on the Statecraft sheet.
