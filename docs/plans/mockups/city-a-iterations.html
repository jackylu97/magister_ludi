Three ways to buy, and to spend

The user chose Arrangement A and asked for iterations on two problems: the Buy button under the queue reads as rushing the item, which it is not, and the Spend card is awkward. Three iterations follow, each with a different answer to both, keeping everything else from A.

Recommendation

A2: Build and Buy as two views of the work rail. The Buy view has no progress bars, every button says "Buy outright", and it lists gold, faith and citizens as three sections with one cost column, which also replaces the Spend card. Borrow one piece of A3: a row's hover card prints its purchase line, so the price can be checked while choosing what to build. If the user wants buying in one click from the build list, A1 is the safe second.

Settled before the iterations

The treasury is on screen

In all three the band carries Treasury 437 (+21) · Faith 51 (+2) beside the six chips, in the top bar's voice. Each iteration shows the bank once more where money is spent.

The hover card is bottom-left

The town rail stops about 180 px short of the bottom and the terrain card sits under it, in the rail's own column, covering no board. Leave and the caption have the bottom centre to themselves.

Detail only on hover, over the right column

A build row's "would add" figures move into the row's hover card, and a chip's breakdown stays a hover card. Both open over the work rail. Rows become one line: name, hammers, turns.

What a purchase is

From src/sim/purchase.ts: a purchase costs the item's full production cost at 4 gold a hammer (or its faith price), plus any card riders, and "does not consume the banked basket and is not discounted by it". Buying a queued item strikes the queued copy off, and the hammers already in it carry on to the next item. The real rush is a different verb, contribute (explainContribution): a card lets you pour coin into the front item's bar, and it stops at what is left. So: a Buy price never sits next to a bar and always says "outright"; contribute keeps the bar and says "→ +N hammers".

A, as drawn

For reference. The Buy button on the "Building now" card, under the bar, is what read as rushing; the Spend card at the foot of the work rail is what read as awkward; the chip breakdown drawn open looked inline.

  • No price on "Building now" or the queue. Every add-list row has a price cell in one column headed "Buy outright · full price".

Recommendation

A2. It is the only one where the objection cannot arise at all: the Buy view has no bars and every button says "Buy outright". The same view answers the Spend card: gold, faith and citizens become one list with one cost column. "What can I buy now?" is one glance at its first section, and the build list loses its prices and gets shorter. Remembering the view avoids Civ VII's complaint. Add A3's purchase line to the row's hover card so the price is visible while choosing. If the user wants one-click buying from the build list, A1 is the safe second.
