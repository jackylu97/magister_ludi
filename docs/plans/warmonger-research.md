# Warmongers in other games: research for Dominion's goals

Research for the user's question (2026-10-14): *"lets think about the goals of the dominion player. could you look across 4x games at warmonger playstyles and bonuses? i want to think through what other games do."* This is a brainstorm, not a ruling; nothing here changes the game until the user rules on it.

Five research passes, each checked against wikis, developer notes, rulebooks and reviews (claims the researchers could not verify are left out or marked):
- **The Civilization series:** IV (Beyond the Sword), V (Brave New World), VI (Gathering Storm and later modes), VII (2025).
- **Recent historical 4X games:** Humankind, Old World, Millennia, Ara: History Untold, Knights of Honor II.
- **Fantasy and science-fiction 4X games:** Endless Legend 1 and 2, Endless Space 2, Age of Wonders 3 and 4, Stellaris, Alpha Centauri, Master of Orion II, Galactic Civilizations.
- **Grand strategy and Total War:** Europa Universalis IV, Crusader Kings III, Imperator: Rome, Total War: Rome II, Attila, Three Kingdoms, Warhammer II and III.
- **Board and card games:** Through the Ages, Twilight Imperium 4, Blood Rage, Inis, Kemet, Cyclades, Root, Eclipse, Clash of Cultures, Tapestry, Dune: Imperium, 7 Wonders, Rising Sun, Scythe, Small World.

## The short version

1. **Every game's warmonger follows the same arc:** fight the wild, take towns, digest them, decide the game. The games differ in which step of that arc their iconic bonuses make fun.
2. **Holding land, not winning battles, is the real limit everywhere:** unrest, loyalty, overextension, settlement limits, war weariness. The bonuses players value most ease that limit. Our game has almost no brake on war: declaring is free, there is no war weariness (deferred in v1), and the user's playtest found that authority stops binding by turn 50. Until a brake binds, cards that ease holding do nothing, and the warmonger's middle game has no puzzle.
3. **Fighting pays a currency that is spent on more war.** The best-remembered war identities work this way: Khorne's skulls, the Hissho's honour, Old World's Assyria (orders per kill), Millennia's Warfare points, Civilization V's Honor (culture per kill). Ours has a ready-made one: culture buys drafts, so a war that pays culture buys cards.
4. **Keep it or cash it out.** Every grand strategy game lets a conqueror either keep a town or cash it out: sack, loot, raze, enslave. Our game has razing, but a razed town pays nothing, so the choice is empty.
5. **Taking things from the enemy is the most memorable payout:** units that change sides, stolen technology, prisoners moved to your capital, seized buildings. It is also the most rule-breaking, so games limit it by chance, by a cap, or to once per age.
6. **Heroes and veterans make the stories.** Players remember Old World's chained attacks, named armies in Rome II, and Civilization VII's commanders carried across ages. They treat plain percentages as compulsory and forgettable, and stacked combat bonuses make battles foregone (a Civilization VII complaint).
7. **Losing should pay something, never as much as winning.** Blood Rage, Kemet, Eclipse and Dune pay the loser, which makes players fight boldly. Blood Rage's Loki strategy shows what happens when losing pays too well. This supports the user's sacrifice loop, with a guardrail.
8. **Targets turn war into quests:** EU4's missions, the Dwarfs' grudges, the Ogres' contracts, Twilight Imperium's secret objectives. That is our commission shape.

## The warmonger's arc in other games

| Phase | What the warmonger tries to do | What stops them | What the iconic bonuses do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Early** | Fight the wild for income; rush a neighbour before walls and defensive units arrive; raid. | The rush window closes (Rome's Praetorians: "their glory is short lived" once Longbowmen arrive, Civilization IV). | Turn barbarians into income: culture per kill (Civ V Honor), a camp pays like a ruin (Civ VI Sumeria), beaten barbarians join you (Civ V Germany), barbarians turned on a rival (Civ VI's Clans, Stellaris' Marauders, Millennia's Khans), raids without war (CK3). |
| **Middle** | Take towns past their walls; make the first captures count; raise heroes. | Walls and sieges; war weariness or war support; holding costs. | Siege tricks (Humankind's free siege engines after a long siege, Kemet's Open Gates, Wulfrik assaulting walls without engines); chained attacks (Old World's Rout and Launch Offensive); the conquering unit promoted (Civ VI Zulu); the first capture of each town pays a technology (Assyria in Civ V and VII). |
| **Digesting** | Hold more land than the limits allow; make each conquest pay for the next. | Overextension, unrest, loyalty, coalitions, settlement limits. | Ease the limit (Civ VI Ottomans keep population, Xerxes' extra settlement per age, Civ VII's "no unrest"); turn conquests into the next army (EU4's Janissaries, raised only in conquered provinces; Imperator's captives moved to the capital). |
| **Late** | Decide the game: take capitals, stop the leader. | Coalitions; everyone else's armies. | Targets on the leader (Twilight Imperium: "win a combat against the player who has the most victory points"); a capital as an instant win (7 Wonders Duel). |

## Patterns across all five groups

1. **Holding is the limit, and the valued bonuses ease it.** Civilization (happiness, loyalty, settlement limit), EU4 (overextension, aggressive expansion), Humankind (occupy now, claim at the peace), Millennia (vassals that must be integrated), Age of Wonders 4 (city slots push conquests into vassals). Players accept a limit they can read and manage. They resent one that feels arbitrary or scales with the whole map (EU4 coalitions over one province; Warhammer III's grudge totals), or that stacks forever (Civilization VII: each razing gives every empire war support against you for good).
2. **Violence pays a war currency.** Skulls from battles, even lost ones (Khorne); honour lost by retreating (the Hissho); War Spoils spent to keep captured enemy units (Age of Wonders 4's Reavers); cadavers turned into food (Endless Legend's Necrophages); orders per kill (Old World's Assyria); prestige and growth for every turn in combat, win or lose (Ara's Itzcoatl).
3. **Keep it or cash it out.** Occupy, loot, sack or raze (Total War); annex, puppet or raze (Civilization V); razing for points (EU4's hordes); razing for permanent income at the price of never holding a town (Age of Wonders 4's Chosen Destroyers); a razed town planting a herdstone that raises army caps (Warhammer's Beastmen).
4. **Taking from the enemy.** Units join you (Civ V Germany, Civ VI Mongolia, Genghis Khan once per age in Civ VII, Boudica, Twilight Imperium's Vuil'raith Cabal). Technology (Assyria; Twilight Imperium's Nekro Virus). People (Imperator's enslavement; Stellaris' Despoilers). Generals (Three Kingdoms). Buildings (Twilight Imperium's Infiltrate). It is limited by chance (Germany: two times in three), by a cap, or to once per age.
5. **Idle armies lose something, and clocks keep the army moving.** Greenskin fightiness drains when idle; Prussian militarisation rises at war and drains at peace; horde unity decays; Craver planets deplete; Clausewitz's Legacy lasts 50 turns. This matches Dominion's "strong now, fading".
6. **The army at peace gets a job through garrisons.** Civilization IV's Hereditary Rule, V's Military Caste, VI's Retainers and Limitanei, VII's commanders stationed in towns, Millennia's units lowering unrest. Dominion's garrison theme is the best-supported part of the deck.
7. **Experience moved to a few carried heroes.** Civilization VII's commanders are the only units that gain experience, and they keep it across ages; players say "less micro, more strategy". Moving many units is the most common tedium complaint (Civilization IV's stacks, Civilization V, Humankind's hordes).
8. **Percentages are required, not remembered.** EU4's military idea groups feel mandatory rather than exciting; Civilization VII's stacked bonuses (+14 to +23 for a dedicated attacker) make tactics "irrelevant". What players talk about breaks a rule (assault walls without siege engines, no attrition, a faction that cannot settle) or adds an action (raid, plant a cove, hunt a monster).
9. **A reason to fight is itself a resource** in games that price war (claims, grievances, casus belli), and war factions get it free. Our game puts no price on declaring war, so this axis is open.
10. **Losing pays where fighting is wanted.** Blood Rage (Valhalla, Loki's cards), Kemet (spent battle cards come back), Eclipse (every side draws reputation), Dune: Imperium (second place pays), Rising Sun (the winner pays its bids to the losers). Where losing pays nothing (Through the Ages, 7 Wonders), military becomes a defensive tax. Two guardrails:
    - Losing must not pay more than winning (Blood Rage's Loki strategy, "extremely hard to beat"; Cyclades' monsters).
    - A kill payout must cover the battle's own losses. Endless Legend 2 players called the Necrophages' corpse economy "non-functional" while the faction held 40% of wins.
11. **Hidden one-off tricks make the drama.** Intrigue cards (Dune), ambushes (Root), traps (Tapestry), action cards (Twilight Imperium), battle cards that cycle (Kemet), one-off invasion tactics (Galactic Civilizations), generals' once-a-fight orders (Old World).
12. **Stakes rise with the ages** (7 Wonders' 1, 3 and 5 points; Blood Rage's 2, 3 and 4 glory), and **early bonuses fade but can leave something behind** (Humankind's legacy traits). Civilization VI replaces its early military policy cards with later ones, as our current-band rule does.

## Our game, for comparison

- **Has:**
  - War is declared freely, with no cost and no casus belli (`src/sim/diplomacy.ts`). Peace offers can carry gold, tribute and towns, and a ten-turn truce follows.
  - A captured town is puppeted by default, annexed, or razed at once; capitals cannot be razed (`docs/war-diplomacy.md`).
  - Towns are taken in three beats (walls, garrison, entry), much like Old World. Surrounding a town starves it (Siegecraft).
  - Authority and happiness are the holding limits.
  - Barbarians fight everyone.
  - Great generals have a +3 aura over two hexes, and build citadels.
  - Levies exist.
  - Bot Dominion seats were at war on 32% to 45% of their turns.
- **Lacks:**
  - Any cost to being at war (war weariness was deferred in v1).
  - A reason to raze, since a razed town pays nothing.
  - Unit experience or veterans.
  - A way to take enemy units, except Blood Brothers on barbarians.
  - A Tier 0 or Tier I card that reads captures or attacks on towns. The first are Siege Works, Triumphs and Hegemony at Tier II.
- **The user's playtest (2026-10-12):** authority stopped being a real limit by turn 50, and midgame drafts did not feel impactful.

## What this suggests for Dominion's goals (a proposal for discussion)

**Age 1 (band 0): the wild is the war.**
- Goal: secure three or four towns, and turn the early army into progress. Camps and barbarian kills pay culture (drafts) and production. Take a weak neighbour's town before walls if one is there.
- On the sheet: Blood Brothers, Blooded Spears, Clear the Frontier, The Cattle Raid, The Funeral Games, and the user's culture-per-kill and faster-camps ideas. This is close to Civilization V's Honor, which players remember.
- Missing: pointing the wild at a rival (Civ VI's Clans, Stellaris' Marauders, Millennia's Khans), and raids without war (CK3).

**Age 2 (band 1): break the first walls, and decide what each town is for.**
- Goal: take the first town against walls and starvation, make the capture count, and raise a first general.
- On the sheet: nothing. Tier I has no siege card and no capture card. This is where the user's "midgame options don't seem impactful" lands.
- From other games:
  - free siege engines after a long siege (Humankind);
  - ignoring walls once (Kemet, Wulfrik; our Wooden Horse idea);
  - a starving town surrendering (our Keys of the City idea);
  - the conquering unit promoted (Civ VI Zulu);
  - the first capture of each town taking a technology (Assyria);
  - razing that pays (our Sack idea; Total War).

**Age 3 (band 2): digest, and make each conquest pay for the next.**
- Goal: hold more towns than the limits allow, turn captured towns into the next army, and grow veterans and generals.
- On the sheet: capture payouts (Triumphs, Conquered Archives, Hegemony, Take the City) and two siege cards (Siege Works, The Siege Train).
- Missing:
  - a loop where captured towns feed the army (EU4's Janissaries; Imperator's captives moved to the capital);
  - unit growth (Millennia's veterans promoted to generals).
- Depends on authority and happiness binding by then. That is a rules question outside the decks, noted here rather than proposed.

**Age 4 (bands 3 and 4): decide the game.**
- Goal: stop or steal the Magnum Opus, and take capitals.
- On the sheet: Spolia, Imperium, Hold the Capitals, The Grand Campaign, The Captured Marvel. This is the best-covered age.
- From other games: hunting the leader (Twilight Imperium's secret objective).

**Across the ages:**
- **The army at peace:** garrisons (the best-supported theme), war by proxy (barbarians turned on a rival), deterrence (attrition on invaders).
- **Possible throughlines beyond the four on the deck map:** sacrifice (Blood Rage), the barbarian lord (Millennia's Khans, Stellaris' Marauders), veterans and heroes (Civilization VII's commanders).

## What players dislike

- Stacked flat bonuses that make battles foregone (Civilization VII), and percentage bonuses that feel mandatory (EU4).
- Moving many units (Civilization IV's stacks, Civilization V, Humankind's hordes).
- Kill payouts smaller than the battle's own losses (Endless Legend 2).
- Losing that pays more than winning (Blood Rage's Loki strategy, Cyclades' monsters).
- Limits that feel arbitrary, scale with the whole map, or stack forever (EU4, Warhammer III, Civilization VII).
- Conditions out of reach (the Ogres' contracts): the same trap as Established Routes.
- Conquest made trivial by a weak AI (Civilization VI), and wars that end once one army is wiped out (Humankind).
- Auto-resolved battles, which leave only the choices made before the fight (Millennia, Ara).

## Game by game

### Civilization
- **IV:**
  - A Great General can be attached as a Warlord (the units with him share experience), settled as an instructor (+2 experience for every unit built there), or spent on an academy.
  - Nationhood drafts citizens into units.
  - The Statue of Zeus doubles the enemy's war weariness.
  - The "stack of doom" is the best-remembered complaint.
  - [Civics guide](https://civfanatics.com/civ4/strategy/introductory/a-guide-to-civics/)
- **V:**
  - Honor gives culture per barbarian kill, reveals camps, and pays gold per kill once finished.
  - Autocracy's Clausewitz's Legacy gives +25% attack for 50 turns.
  - Assyria takes a technology from each conquered town. The Aztecs get culture per kill. Germany's beaten barbarians join two times in three. The Songhai get triple pillage gold. The Ottomans capture beaten ships.
  - A great general's citadel seizes the hexes around it, even foreign ones.
  - A captured town can be annexed, puppeted or razed.
  - [Honor](https://www.carlsguides.com/strategy/civilization5/socialpolicies/honor.php), [Annex, puppet, raze](https://carlsguides.com/strategy/civilization5/war/annex-puppet-raze.php)
- **VI:**
  - Era-tied military policy cards (Agoge, Raid, Retainers, Limitanei) are replaced by later ones.
  - Macedon has no war weariness, and capturing towns with certain districts advances its research.
  - The Zulu unit that conquers a town becomes a corps. Mongolia and the Aztec Eagle Warrior capture beaten units.
  - Sumeria's cleared barbarian outposts pay like a village. Hungary's levied units arrive stronger.
  - The Barbarian Clans mode lets you hire, bribe, incite or raid a clan.
  - [Civilopedia](https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/governments/)
- **VII:**
  - Commanders are the only units that gain experience, and they keep it across ages.
  - There is a settlement limit and war support. The Militaristic tree removes unrest in captured settlements.
  - Xerxes gains culture and gold on each first capture, and +1 settlement limit per age. Assyria takes a technology and a relic per first capture. Mongolia gets free cavalry per captured settlement. Genghis Khan's commander converts adjacent independents once per age.
  - Complaints: stacked bonuses, and a razing penalty that never fades.
  - [Combat diary](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-guide/dev-diary/combat/), [Militaristic](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Militaristic_(Civ7))

### Recent historical 4X
- **Humankind:**
  - War support is tracked for each pair of empires. You occupy towns during the war and claim them at the peace.
  - A siege held long enough brings free siege engines.
  - Militarists raise upkeep-free militia once a turn and may declare war without a grievance.
  - Legacy traits keep a small bonus after a culture is left behind.
  - [Amplitude](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/humankind/blogs/962)
- **Old World:**
  - One pool of orders pays for every action, and unused orders become gold, so war spends what peace would bank.
  - Assyria gains orders per kill.
  - Rout lets a unit attack again after a kill; it was patched twice to limit chains.
  - Generals have once-a-fight orders (Launch Offensive, a stun).
  - Carthage hires tribe mercenaries at a rising price.
  - [Update 88](https://mohawkgames.com/2022/04/13/old-world-update-88/)
- **Millennia:**
  - Fighting enough between the second and third ages can tip the whole world into the Age of Blood.
  - Warfare points from fighting buy forced marches, healing and upgrades.
  - Veterans can be promoted to leaders, and outdated leaders retire for points.
  - The Raiders spirit is "an army printer button" that fades before gunpowder. The Khans spirit turns barbarians into allies.
  - [Ages](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/millennia/news/ages-part-one)
- **Ara: History Untold:** leaders whose traits pay for fighting (Itzcoatl: every turn in combat), for winning a war (Sennacherib: faster building for ten turns), and for time since a defensive war (Boudicca). [Dev journal 5](https://www.stardock.com/games/article/538266/ara-dev-journal-5-the-great-differentiation)

### Fantasy and science fiction
- **Endless Legend:** the Necrophages cannot make peace and gain attack per declared war; the Cultists convert other empires' villages; razing pays by population. Endless Legend 2's corpse economy drew complaints even while the faction was winning. [Traits](https://endlesslegend.wiki.gg/wiki/Traits), [Endless Legend 2 thread](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/endless-legend-2/forums/360-endless-legend-2/threads/59624-the-issues-with-the-necrophage-faction)
- **Endless Space 2:** the Cravers must keep taking worlds because theirs deplete; the Hissho earn honour from battles, lose it by retreating, and spend it to grow. [Hissho](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/endless-space-2/blogs/626-supremacy-hissho-faction)
- **Age of Wonders 4:** grievances are banked reasons for war that can be earned, fabricated, sold or forgiven; Reaver War Spoils from kills and captures keep captured units; Chosen Destroyers raze for permanent income and can never hold a town. [Diplomacy](https://aow4.paradoxwikis.com/Diplomacy), [Reaver](https://aow4.paradoxwikis.com/Reaver)
- **Stellaris:** claims cost influence; war exhaustion can force a peace; Barbaric Despoilers abduct population; Marauders can be paid off, paid to raid a rival, or hired. [Dev diary 101](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/stellaris-dev-diary-101-marauders-pirates-and-the-horde.1065594)
- **Others:**
  - In Alpha Centauri, units abroad make citizens unhappy at home.
  - Master of Orion II captures ships by boarding and planets by mind control.
  - Galactic Civilizations sells one-off invasion tactics that damage the prize. In Galactic Civilizations III's Crusade, a dead soldier is a lost citizen.
  - [Spartans](https://alphacentauri2.com/wiki/Spartan_Federation), [Crusade](https://www.stardock.com/games/article/482685/preview-guide-galactic-civilizations-iii-crusade)

### Grand strategy and Total War
- **EU4:**
  - Aggressive expansion and coalitions, overextension, and war exhaustion limit conquest.
  - Prussian militarisation rises at war and drains at peace.
  - Ottoman Janissaries come only from conquered provinces of another faith.
  - Hordes raze for points and must keep raiding.
  - [Aggressive expansion](https://eu4.paradoxwikis.com/Aggressive_expansion)
- **CK3:** raiding without war, with the loot lost if the raiders are caught on the way home; adventurers refill their troops from sieges; tributaries; Struggles switch conquest on and off. [Raiding](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Raiding)
- **Imperator:** cohorts grow loyal to their general in battle, which can start a civil war; a general can be retired with a Triumph; sieges led by a commander move captives to the capital. [Civil war](https://imperator.paradoxwikis.com/Civil_war), [Enslavement](https://imperator.paradoxwikis.com/Enslavement)
- **Total War:**
  - Every captured town can be occupied, looted, sacked or razed.
  - Rome II's named armies keep their traditions when rebuilt. Three Kingdoms lets you recruit captured generals.
  - In Warhammer:
    - the Beastmen raze to raise army caps;
    - the Skaven plant hidden under-cities;
    - the Dwarfs settle grudges;
    - the Ogres take contracts;
    - Greenskin fightiness drains when idle;
    - Khorne's skulls pay even from defeats;
    - Wulfrik assaults walls without siege engines.
  - [Rome II sieges](https://r2encv2.totalwar.com/en/manual/single-player/0021_enc_page_campaign_play_military_sieges/index.html)

### Board and card games
- **Through the Ages:** aggressions steal from weaker players; wars are declared a turn ahead and pay by the gap in strength; colonies are bought by sacrificing units. Military is often called a defensive tax. [Rulebook](https://rulespal.com/through-the-ages/rulebook)
- **Twilight Imperium 4:** secret objectives that need a war ("win a combat against the player who has the most victory points"; "lose control of a planet in a home system"); factions that capture destroyed units and rebuild them free, steal technology, or take the loser's command token. [Yin Brotherhood](https://scottmk.github.io/ti4-reference/factions/base/yin/)
- **Blood Rage:** dead figures go to Valhalla and come back next age; Loki's cards pay for losing battles, and the Loki strategy is called "extremely hard to beat". [Loki](https://www.skeletoncodemachine.com/p/losing-on-purpose-with-the-loki-strategy)
- **Kemet:** only winning attackers earn permanent points; Open Gates ignores walls; battle cards come back once all are spent. [Rules](https://www.ultraboardgames.com/kemet/game-rules.php)
- **Others:**
  - Inis' military win counts living enemy clans, so peace beats annihilation.
  - Root's Eyrie must carry out a growing decree every turn.
  - Eclipse pays reputation to every side of a battle.
  - Dune: Imperium pays second place.
  - In Rising Sun the winner pays its bids to the losers.
  - 7 Wonders scores 1, 3, then 5 points per age.
  - [Eyrie](https://therootdatabase.com/law/eyrie-dynasties/en), [Dune diary](https://news.direwolfdigital.com/dune-imperium-designer-diary-3-conflicts/)
