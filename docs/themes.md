# The Themes — reconciliation ledger

The game's content answers to four unrelated tag vocabularies (see `docs/codex.md`). This
doc is the reconciliation: the gameplay themes that **already exist** in the pool, derived
from what the cards actually do, written plainly. Each entry: the fantasy, the loop, where
it lives today, and its state. The `tags:` line maps today's internal vocabulary onto the
theme so the merge can be mechanical later. Next step (the user's): add/cut themes, then
give each an identity across the ages.

---

## 1. The War Economy
Soldiers are an economy, not an expense. Units pay yields by being built (faith per unit),
by dying (production + gold to the widow, culture in verse), and by killing (culture,
faith, healing). The loop: cheap units in → events out. The completest engine in the pool
and entirely undiscoverable today.
- Lives in: Orders (Conscription, The Widow's Levy, Rites of Passage), Doctrines (The Iron
  Price, The Standing Army), beliefs (God of the Forge, Rites of Blood), a tech (Epic
  Poetry), beads (The Conqueror, The Strongest Arm).
- State: **complete but hidden** — no wonder, no great-person hook, no named identity.
- tags: forge · war · domination

## 2. The Wild Frontier
The steppe as a harvest: camps found, camps cleared, barbarians killed or tamed — each
pays. Wolf-Mother's Pact flips the whole rule (barbarians join you). Overlaps the War
Economy early, then has nowhere to go once the wild thins.
- Lives in: Orders (Camp Followers, Spoils of the Wild, Border Ballads), one Doctrine
  (Wolf-Mother's Pact), beliefs (Lady of the Hunt).
- State: **fun, early-only** — dies by Æra III with no heir; no beads, no wonder.
- tags: hunt · wild

## 3. The Pilgrim Engine
Faith-per-turn as the master resource, converted into everything else: culture (The Great
Litany, Lamplighters), gold (The Tithe), science (Divine Inspiration, The Curia), happiness
(Pilgrim Roads). The loop: stack 🕯/turn → route it through converters → spend the rest on
augurs and rites.
- Lives in: Orders/Doctrines (the whole procession line), governments (Theocracy, The
  Curia), enhancers (Reliquaries), beads (The Hierophant, The Tithe).
- State: **complete** — the best-supported engine; arguably over-supplied with converters.
- tags: procession · stone(part) · sun(part)

## 4. The Mission
Faith pointed *outward*: convert foreign cities and be paid per foreign follower (Apostles,
World Church, Congregation, The Long Prayer, Pilgrims' Coin). The loop: pressure out →
followers abroad → yields home. Distinct from the Pilgrim Engine: that one banks faith,
this one exports it.
- Lives in: enhancer beliefs almost entirely, plus beads (The Apostle, The Widest Faith)
  and two edge Doctrines (Religious Mandate, Sanctuary).
- State: **one-channel** — no Orders, no wonder-identity beyond Hagia Sophia's pressure,
  no great-person hook past the prophet.
- tags: road(part) · the enhancer pool

## 5. The Caravan State
Roads and routes as the empire's veins: gold per route, routes pay more, routes carry
extra cargo (faith both ways, a beaker each). The loop: connect everything → per-route and
per-connection payoffs → gold buys what hammers can't.
- Lives in: Orders (Silk Roads, Ledger-Keepers), Doctrines (The Sea Charter), a government
  (Merchant League), techs (The Imperial Post, The Knotted Cord), enhancers (The Long Road,
  Sacred Cartography — the best crossover cards in the game), beads (The Grand Caravan,
  The Richest Roads, The Road-Builder).
- State: **complete and currently degenerate** (the 300-gpt stack) — needs a trigger and a
  trim, not more adders.
- tags: caravan · road(part) · economic

## 6. The Luxury Table
Luxuries as a collection game: gold and happiness per unique kind, per copy, per improved
resource (Salt Tithes, Sumptuary Laws, Village Fairs, Provincial Mints, The Grand Bazaar).
The loop: claim/buy/trade for kinds → happiness becomes the scaling ceiling-lifter. This is
the natural **wide-play happiness engine** Entry LII calls for — it already half-exists.
- Lives in: Orders/Doctrines (caravan line's other half), beliefs (Lord of the Hoard,
  Pilgrimage), a wonder (Temple of Artemis's shape).
- State: **half-built** — capped by map scarcity by design; the duplicate-copies rule
  (Grand Bazaar, Provincial Mints) is the lever to extend it.
- tags: caravan(part) · green(part)

## 7. The Land Rush
Ground itself as the payoff: cheaper hexes, faster borders, cheaper settlers, cities born
grown (Land Grants, Royal Surveyors, Homestead Charters, Manifest of the Steppe, The
Founders' Road, Charter Towns), and authority capacity as the wide-play fuel (Hegemony,
Client Kings, Provincial Governors). The loop: authority + border culture → more ground →
more of everything.
- Lives in: Orders (mostly `line:none` today — the unnamed cluster), Doctrines, a tech
  (Colonial Charters), beads (The Founder, The Twelve, The Most Cities, The Surveyor).
- State: **rich but anonymous** — the biggest untagged cluster; The Charter posture's spine.
- tags: none(most) · charter · green(part)

## 8. The Tall Hearth
Population as the multiplier: food kept on growth (Public Granaries, Common Table),
per-citizen yields (Census of Souls, Statute Labour, Choirs, The Tax Farm), thresholds for
big cities (Pax Imperia, The Grain Dole, Ancestor Worship), and the guilds turning surplus
citizens into specialists. The loop: food → citizens → per-head yields → science and
culture compound.
- Lives in: everywhere — Orders, Doctrines, governments (Republic, The Estates), follower
  beliefs, guilds, beads (The Metropolis, The Greatest City, The Census of the World).
- State: **complete** — and the beads' current bias toward it is the endeavour problem.
- tags: green · hearth · ploughshare

## 9. The Court of Great People
Renown as a currency and legacies as a collection: call people faster (The Laureate),
legacies stronger (The Renaissance Court), buy people outright (The Commonwealth), plant
works that pay (citadel/academy/works, The Patron). The loop: renown buildings + Triumphs
→ people → permanent legacies stacking into a late-game deck of their own.
- Lives in: Orders/Doctrines (court — one card today), governments (t45 pair), beads (The
  Dynasty, The Most Called, the First-X feats), wonders indirectly.
- State: **system built, deck thin** — one `court` Order exists; the family tags
  (scholar/merchant/artist/engineer/general) should dissolve into the other themes.
- tags: court · the GP family vocabulary

## 10. The Observatory
Science tempo as identity: payoffs per technology completed (The Lyceum, The Bronze
Mirror), per age reached (Rites of Blood's scaling, ruin finds), age-lead as a weapon (The
Scholar's Wager, First Into the Age). The loop: beakers → age lead → everything that
scales `perAge` pays more, and the cost bands punish laggards.
- Lives in: Orders (star line), one Doctrine (The Encyclopaedia), beads (The Deepest
  Learning), wonders (Great Library, House of Wisdom).
- State: **thin as a theme** — mostly flat science adders; the per-age/per-tech scaling
  shape is its real identity and barely used.
- tags: star · sky(part) · science

## 11. The Cartographers
The unknown as a resource: ruins pay (Curious Elders, Oracle of the Crossroads, Athenaeum
of the Road), revealed hexes pay (The Cartographers), sight and movement stack (Far
Runners, Master of Maps). The loop: scouts out → one-time finds + map knowledge → an
information economy. Naturally hands off to the sea (ocean-going, circumnavigation).
- Lives in: Orders (wayfarers), Doctrines (Master of Maps, Athenaeum), beliefs (Keeper of
  the Calendar), the explorer units.
- State: **early-only today** — like the Wild it dies by Æra III; the naval line is its
  obvious second act and isn't wired to it yet.
- tags: wayfarers · road(part)

## 12. The Stoneworks
Hammers and monuments as the identity: payoffs per building completed (Master Masons), per
building standing (The Encyclopaedia), wonder-holding as an engine (The Grand Tour, the
Exposition), windfalls doubled (The Woodwrights, chops), production thresholds (The
Forge-City). The loop: hammers → buildings/wonders → per-stone payoffs → more hammers.
- Lives in: Orders (forge's non-military half — Master Masons, Quarrymen's Guild, Statute
  Labour), beliefs (Guild of the Faithful, The Standing Stones), beads (building-in-every-
  city endeavours, The Most Marvels, The Builder), all 27 wonders as its trophies.
- State: **split across tags** — forge mixes it with the War Economy; worth naming apart.
- tags: forge(part) · stone · culture(beads)

## 13. The Waters
Coast and river as a siting bet: coastal cities pay and cost less authority
(Thalassocracy, Mare Nostrum, Harbour Dues), fresh water doubles down (River Kings, River
Mother, Cistern Works), water hexes pay (The Floating Fields, Lord of the Sea). The loop:
found on water → siting payoffs → the naval line defends the investment.
- Lives in: Doctrines (the strongest cards here), beliefs (water axis), techs (Floating
  Fields), the naval roster.
- State: **payoffs without a game** — good cards, but nothing to *do* at sea yet beyond
  them; blockade + plunder are the missing verbs.
- tags: water · caravan(part) · frost/sun (climate-siting cousins)

---

**Cross-cutting observations for the unification pass:**
- Complete engines: 1, 3, 5, 8. Thin or one-channel: 4, 9, 10, 11, 13. Early-only: 2, 11.
  Anonymous: 7. Split: 12 (out of forge), 6 (out of caravan).
- The best cards in the pool are **crossovers** (Sacred Cartography: trade×faith; The
  Knotted Cord: trade×science; Pilgrimage: luxury×faith) — the unified vocabulary should
  keep dual-theme cards legal, tagged with a primary.
- Every theme should eventually name: its trigger, its payoff, its scaler (Entry LII's
  triangle), its wonder, its great-person hook, its bead card, and its age-by-age identity
  — that grid is the next page of this doc, after the theme list is ratified.
