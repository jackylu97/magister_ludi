# Bot play audit, 21 November 2026

The worker change gave the clearest gain on maps held out from tuning. War declarations were a poor predictor of conquest. A single increase to deck yield weights moved different decks in opposite directions.

### Can Mali improve by settling or defending differently?

The second round kept normal difficulty and changed only Mansa Musa's settings. Alexander and Sejong retained their normal settings. Every comparison used the same map and starting seats. We tried a higher city value on two screening maps and checked it on two other maps. We then tried a stronger preference for early settlements that faded as Mali gained cities, with seed 431 as an additional check. The defensive army mix trial followed a replay of the loss of Timbuktu.

| Map seed | Standard score | Higher city value | Higher value for early cities | More ranged defenders |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 133 | 200 | 133 | 162 |
| 2026 | 286 | 887 | 1,034 | 265 |
| 777 | 732 | 278 | 672 | 640 |
| 1907 | 293 | 288 | 287 | 338 |
| 431 | 97 | Untested | 97 | 149 |

Scores are at victory or turn 100. On seed 5 the higher city value game ended on turn 99. At the common turn 80 on that map, Mali had four cities, 27 net gold per turn and 225 score, against one city, five gold and 152 score in the control.

- **Settlement.** Raising `weights.city` from 200 to 250 helped on both screening maps. It reduced final score on both holdouts. On seed 777, Mali went from seven cities and 79 net gold per turn to five cities and a net loss of three gold per turn. With the same city value and the per-city multiplier lowered from 0.90 to 0.83, most of that loss disappeared. That version beat the control on just one of five maps.
- **Defense.** Raising the preferred army from two to three units per city cost Mali a city earlier on seed 5 and improved its score on seed 2026. Increasing the ranged share of a defensive army from 30% to 55% raised the final score on three of five maps. Mali still lost all its cities on both maps where this happened in the control. Keep the usual army size and mix.
- **The loss of Timbuktu.** On seed 5 Alexander took the capital on turn 64. At the start of that turn, Mali had 11 combat units, including a Warrior garrison. Five Warriors and one Archer stood within two hexes of Timbuktu, counting the garrison. Alexander had four Bowmen and one Warrior in that radius. Mali was already building an Archer. Its last city fell on turn 90, when it had only one combat unit left. A command-by-command replay showed that Timbuktu opened turn 64 with one city hit point and a 30-HP garrison. A Bowman removed the garrison, then a Warrior took the city before Mali issued a local unit order. On turn 62 a Warrior that fortified had two legal Bowman targets. Either attack forecast 18 damage dealt and 50 taken. An earlier reinforcement or a move to block the capturing Warrior is a more specific next experiment.

The follow-up branch `codex/bot-mali-trials` also fixes the appraisal of Orders that unlock multiple kinds of faith purchase. The Gifts of the Faithful could count the same faith income toward culture buildings and toward faith buildings. The bot now prices the best purchase that income can fund. On seeds 5 and 777 the corrected appraisal and the control had identical recorded checkpoints through turn 100. The branch also reuses the fixed list of improvements a technology opens. It still checks each seat's worker settings and unlocks. In twelve paired readings of all 68 technologies for two seats, all 136 explanations matched and the changed code used less CPU in ten pairs. The median paired reduction was 4.6% for this part of a research decision. [Normal-difficulty settings, games and combat trace](../plans/benchmarks/bot-mali-2026-11-21.json)

The audit runner accepts a JSON settings file for a single seat. From the follow-up branch, bundle `scripts/botPlayAudit.ts` as shown below, then run:

### Settlement sites

The settler lens priced fresh water at six points and coast at four. The bot read its own six-point fresh-water bonus and gave coastal access zero direct value. The settlement candidate shares the two defaults and lets an Arena seat override either one, including setting it to zero.

On version 256, 24/12 and 16/8 both produced two more fresh-water expansions on three matched maps. The stricter setting cost 10.9% of total yield and 10.2% of score. The moderate setting kept yield close to baseline (+0.8%) and cost 5.0% of score.

On version 257, I tested 16/8 against the same combined bots on two maps through turn 80. The share of expansion towns with fresh water or coast available at founding rose from 14/28 (50%) to 17/26 (65%). Total yield per turn rose from 1,601.5 to 1,778.1. Total score fell from 2,914 to 2,667 and technologies fell from 114 to 103. Choose 16/8 if the objective is better access to water. The score and research tradeoffs make it a poor default for increasing difficulty. The trial has its own branch, `codex/bot-settlement-water`. [Site results and reproduction steps](../plans/benchmarks/settlement-water-2026-11-21/report.md)

## How the games were scored

The baseline was `e09c6a35`, save version 256. Each game used the normal bot driver, real leader powers, real deck pairs and barbarians. The first three maps used seeds 5, 42 and 777. Seeds 314, 2026 and 13 were held out for checking changes. Each short game had three leaders on a duel-size board. One trio used Alexander, Mansa Musa and Sejong. The other used Mehmed, Lorenzo and Jayavarman. The starting seats rotated across maps and stayed fixed across policies on each map.

Each result stores the official score, research count and deepest research column, highest age, number of culture drafts, all six end-of-turn yield rates, city captures, city count and population. Total yield adds the six rates, including net gold. The sum of rates across turns measures recurring economic output. It excludes one-time awards and differs from the treasury. Culture drafts measure progress along the government ladder. The draft count and culture per turn measure different things.

Turn 80 gives a common checkpoint even on maps where a leader won early. All held-out games reached turn 100. All seats contribute to each mean, including eliminated seats. A leader counts in both its decks. The nine seats on the three held-out maps share three games. The differences are descriptive, and one strong empire can move an average substantially.

## Other experiments

- Increasing each deck's main yield weight by 25% on the first three maps raised Commerce gold but cut total yield by 20.6% against the original baseline at turn 80. Scientific science fell 51.7%, Magisterial culture fell 56.3%, and the nine seats researched one fewer technology on average. Keep the four age weights available for individual experiments.
- Giving techs that open a new age one extra unit of value moved mean research depth from 5.56 to 5.67 columns against the worker change. The tech count stayed at 19.89 and total yield fell from 584.75 to 582.57. Two of three maps had identical checkpoint readings. This test gives little reason to change the default.
- Requiring the Dominion declaration target to match the reachable campaign target produced identical checkpoint readings on all three maps. Giving a guaranteed adjacent capture first choice also reproduced those readings. The latter did change a constructed choice between a city and a nearby barbarian.
- Reducing the Dominion attack group from four spare soldiers to two produced one extra capture by the end of the three screening games. Testing the same setting on the combined candidate across three held-out maps produced zero additional captures. At turn 100, mean score across all nine seats increased from 773 to 824. The three Dominion seats themselves fell from 724 to 684 on average. Their opponents accounted for the gain. Keep the four-unit default.
- On the income experiment, the bot treated a legal second use of a Great Person as a duplicate. The repeat guard compared the command and position without comparing the remaining ability allowance. The candidate corrects the guard for Great People, merchant sales and units that can attack twice. In the reproduced game Sejong still won, on turn 94 instead of 91, and three warnings disappeared.

## Conquest

In the baseline six-leader game on a standard board, Mehmed declared 17 wars and Alexander declared three. Nobody took a city. Mansa Musa won on turn 146. The last 20 full turns took a median of 11.6 seconds and a 95th percentile of 20.4 seconds while other work ran. These timings include every bot and turn resolution.

On the three-seat version of seed 42, Mehmed attacked Florence 53 times and Pisa nine times. Florence ended 15 attacks at one hit point over ten different turns. City health after an attack does not identify whether a garrison still held the city. The code and a separate fixture establish a second problem. A melee capture of an empty city deals zero damage. The regular attack selector recognized the capture in its forecast, then rejected it in the final damage comparison. The candidate accepts a legal capture and keeps its explanation in the spectator. An optional combat value controls when capturing a city also takes precedence over attacking a different target.

The same final attack check was also rejecting zero-damage captures of civilians. I separated the two changes. Fixing only city captures reproduced every worker-only checkpoint on the first three maps. With both fixes, Mehmed took two cities on seed 42 and Alexander lost the capture he had previously made on seed 5. The three held-out maps still had zero captures. A second replay checked melee positions immediately after each of the 15 attacks that left Florence at one hit point. A melee unit stood adjacent in 13 cases. In four cases its combat forecast offered an immediate capture. Other one-hit-point positions still had a garrison. This gives a specific place to inspect the order of ranged and melee actions. The isolated city-only experiment did not reproduce the two natural captures on the worker baseline.

The combined build had mixed economic results. On the held-out maps at turn 100, score rose from 744.6 to 773.0, completed techs rose from 22.7 to 24.0, and drafts rose from 14.8 to 15.6. Total yield per turn fell from 592.9 to 552.3. The sum of recurring rates through turn 100 rose 7.0%. Final science on Scientific seats was 53.6 against 41.8, Magisterial culture 135.8 against 117.2, Commerce gold 29.5 against 37.5, Devotion faith 27.0 against 25.0 and Expansionist food 205.3 against 201.7. The worker results and the combined results answer different questions.

On the matched standard map, the worker change produced a victory for Lorenzo on turn 116. Mehmed captured one city, and three leaders built 14 of the newly considered improvements. The last 20 turns took a median of 44.8 seconds and a 95th percentile of 119.0 seconds while the full test suite ran. The baseline and candidate had different end turns and machine loads. Use an isolated timing run to judge late-turn speed.

Two further games recorded each Order held and each improvement built. Across seeds 5 and 314 the six leaders held deck-specific Orders from all six decks. The counts were 14 Dominion, 13 Expansionist, 11 Commerce, 3 Devotion, 21 Scientific and 24 Magisterial. These counts exclude common and cross-deck Orders. Jayavarman also built a Stone Circle.

## Tuning in the Arena

Select a leader to use its deck pair, abilities and starting bias. Its usual persona is selected automatically. Choose a single seat under **Apply settings to** and the panel reads that persona's actual starting values. Edits to that seat take precedence over its persona. For example, Alexander normally wants four combat units per city. The test changed that to two and left the opponent at its own default. ## Citizen assignments

In the version 256 baseline, all four citizen focus settings ranked tiles by food, production and gold. On otherwise equal tiles the sim can pass over an Academy, Landmark or Stone Circle. The worker also uses this ranking to predict whether a citizen will work an improvement. Adding weights for science, culture and faith changed 12 of 191 sampled city positions. A test town could gain eight science per turn while meeting food upkeep. Without the food safeguard the town would starve.

In a separate two-map, four-leader experiment the guarded change raised combined score from 2,108 to 2,314 and combined final yield rate by 18.2%. The four seats researched 87 techs against 89 before. Claude subsequently shipped a different solution in version 257. It uses age-specific weights for all six yields on default focus, counts the three other yields on manual focuses and protects food upkeep. The trial recorded here is historical. The [citizen study and candidate patch](../plans/benchmarks/citizen-weights-2026-11-21/report.md) contain the city fixtures and all four score lines.

One bot calculation still predicts different results from the sim. The version 257 focus forecast skips the step that chooses enough food to support the town. In an Age IV town with three citizens, the bot predicted that food focus would gain eight food and cost twelve science. Applying the command gained two food and cost four science. The bot chose to keep default focus in this case. Reuse the sim's citizen selection when comparing focuses, then test how often the corrected forecast changes a decision.

## Decisions and next experiments

| Change | Recommendation | What to measure next |
|---|---|---|
| Worker access and repeat guards | Use both. Each fixes an action the rules already allow. | Keep one candidate seat against two unchanged opponents. Track actual use of unlocked works and extra actions. |
| Age-based research and deck weights | Keep the current defaults. | Change one deck and one age at a time. Score the tuned seat and its opponents separately. |
| Zero-damage captures | Use the corrected action check. Keep the extra city capture value at zero by default. | For each breached city, record the nearest available melee unit, its assignment and the number of turns until it can attack. Compare city captures per war and time from breach to capture. |
| Citizen yield weights | Use the version 257 rule already merged by Claude. | Track food deficits, late-game growth and the specialist yields. The rule applies to human towns. |

## Reproduce a comparison

The candidate branch is `codex/bot-arena-trials`. Its `scripts/botPlayAudit.ts` accepts a policy, seed, turn cap, output path, optional seat, map size and roster. Use the same source revision and policy as the result. For a baseline, copy the experiment script alone into a checkout of `e09c6a35`. Bundle it in that checkout so the imports select the matching game rules.

```sh
./node_modules/.bin/esbuild scripts/botPlayAudit.ts --bundle --platform=node --format=esm --target=node20 --outfile=/tmp/bot-play-audit.mjs
node /tmp/bot-play-audit.mjs base 42 100 /tmp/base-42.json
```

The roster-only and income studies used the baseline source and per-seat overrides. The full worker and research studies used the worker patch alone. The repeat-guard and first capture preference were added in `62f2b58b`. The Arena and regular capture candidates are in `d4a8641f`. The [experiment records](../plans/benchmarks/bot-play-2026-11-21.json) identify each comparison, seed and result.
