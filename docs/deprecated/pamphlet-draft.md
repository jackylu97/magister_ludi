# The Pamphlet — the second edition (working draft, 2026-09-14)

A transcript of the pamphlet as it prints today (`src/ui/pamphlet.ts`,
`PAMPHLET_PAGES`), one page per section, with the shot each page carries.
Edit the **second edition** column freely; the first edition stays for
comparison until the rewrite lands. Bold words are Compendium links. The rule
of the page stands: one hero shot, a heading, at most two short sentences.

The shots are still the 2026-09-03 set in `public/pamphlet/` and will be
re-taken against the painted look and the leaders' second cut.

## 1 · Select a unit

*Shot: a settler selected on its hex, with its orders panel open on the right.*

**First edition.**
Left-click a unit on the board — or its entry in the panel — and the panel on the right is where its orders live.
The game is turn-based: take your time, because the world moves only when every player has ended the turn.

**Second edition.**
Click a unit on the map, or its row in the side panel, to select it. Its orders appear in the panel on the right.
Nothing happens until every player has ended the turn, so there is no hurry.

## 2 · Move and attack

*Shot: a combat forecast, shown while hovering an enemy with a unit selected.*

**First edition.**
With a unit selected, right-click a hex to move there, or right-click an enemy to attack.
Hover the enemy first: the forecast shows both sides' **strength** and the damage each would take.

**Second edition.**
With a unit selected, right-click a hex to move there. Right-click an enemy to attack it.
Hover an enemy first to see the forecast: each side's **strength** and how much damage each would take.

## 3 · Found cities, build things

*Shot: the city screen, with a build queue being chosen.*

**First edition.**
A settler founds a **city**; click the city to choose what it builds — an empty queue is a wasted turn.
More cities means a stronger empire, so keep settling — grass and fresh water make the best ground.

**Second edition.**
A settler founds a **city**. Click the city to choose what it builds; a city with nothing queued wastes its turn.
More cities make a stronger empire, so keep settling. Grassland beside fresh water is the best ground.

## 4 · Read the banner

*Shot: a city banner up close: the size number, the growth ring, the build line.*

**First edition.**
The number on a banner is the city's size; the ring around it fills as the city grows.
Under the name: what the city is building, and how many turns remain.

**Second edition.**
The number on a city's banner is its size. The ring around the number fills as the city grows toward its next citizen.
Under the name is what the city is building and how many turns it needs.

## 5 · Read a hex

*Shot: the tile card for a hovered hex, with the yields lens on behind it.*

**First edition.**
Hover any hex to see its **yields** — food grows the city, hammers build, coins keep the army paid.
The yields lens paints every hex's pay straight onto the map.

**Second edition.**
Hover any hex to see what it **yields**. Food grows a city, production builds things, gold pays for your army and buildings.
Turn on the yields lens to see every hex's yields painted on the map at once.

## 6 · The technology tree

*Shot: the technology tree: nodes joined by lines, with a research queue aimed.*

**First edition.**
Each node on the tree is one **technology**; the lines run from what it needs to what it opens.
Click any node to aim at it — the game queues whatever has to come first.

**Second edition.**
Each node on the tree is a **technology**. The lines show what each one needs and what it unlocks.
Click any node to research toward it. The game queues everything that has to come first.

## 7 · Statecraft and religion

*Shot: the Statecraft screen: a drafted card going into a government slot.*

**First edition.**
Culture earns drafts of cards — pick one, then place it in a slot on the **Statecraft** screen to make it law.
Faith works on the **Religion** screen — gods, blessings, and in time a religion of your own.

**Second edition.**
Culture earns you card drafts. Pick a card, then place it in a slot on the **Statecraft** screen to put it into effect.
Faith is spent on the **Religion** screen, on gods and blessings, and later on a religion of your own.

## 8 · Workers improve the land

*Shot: a worker on an owned hex, offering the improvements that hex can take.*

**First edition.**
A worker builds **improvements** — farms, mines, pastures — and is spent over a few uses, so improve your best hexes first.
Clearing a forest pays production once, but the forest's own yield is gone for good — a trade, not a cleanup.

**Second edition.**
A worker builds **improvements** such as farms, mines and pastures. Each worker has a few uses before it is spent, so improve your best hexes first.
Clearing a forest pays production once, but the forest's own yield is gone for good. It is a trade, not a cleanup.

## 9 · The fog and the wild

*Shot: the edge of the known world, with a barbarian camp found in it.*

**First edition.**
You see only what your units can see — the rest waits under the **fog of war**, and exploring it pays: ruins reward whoever arrives first.
Barbarian camps raid until somebody burns them out — keep a soldier at home.

**Second edition.**
You see only what your units and cities can see. The rest is hidden under the **fog of war**, and exploring pays: ruins reward whoever reaches them first.
Barbarian camps send raiders until someone destroys them, so keep a soldier at home.

## 10 · End the turn

*Shot: the End Turn button in its waiting state, naming what it still needs.*

**First edition.**
Press End Turn when you are done; all players move at once, so the world resolves when everyone has pressed it.
If the button balks it is not an error — a unit, a city, or a draft still needs your answer, and pressing it takes you there.

**Second edition.**
Press End Turn when you are done. All players move at the same time, so the turn resolves once everyone has pressed it.
If the button says it is waiting, that is not an error: a unit, a city or a draft still needs a decision, and pressing it takes you there.

## 11 · Happiness and authority

*Shot: the two meter chips on the top bar, beside the six yield figures.*

**First edition.**
**Happiness** limits how big your cities get; authority limits how many you can hold.
Going over is a cost, not a defeat — hover either number to see where it stands.

**Second edition.**
**Happiness** limits how large your cities can grow. Authority limits how many cities you can hold.
Going over either limit costs you, but does not end the game. Hover either number to see where you stand.

## 12 · War and peace

*Shot: the Diplomacy screen, where war is declared and peace is made.*

**First edition.**
You are at peace until somebody declares war — until then their soldiers cannot enter your land, and you cannot **attack** them.
War, peace, and deals live on the Diplomacy screen, behind the flag button.

**Second edition.**
You are at peace with everyone until someone declares war. At peace, their soldiers cannot enter your land and you cannot **attack** them.
War, peace and deals are all on the Diplomacy screen, behind the flag button.

## 13 · How you win

*Shot: the Abacus: every player's beads, strung side by side.*

**First edition.**
The game is scored in **beads** — points for doing things first in the world; twenty beads lets you begin the Magnum Opus.
Finishing that great work wins the game outright, whatever anybody else holds.

**Second edition.**
The game is scored in **beads**, earned by keeping the wagers each age deals you. Enough beads let you begin the Magnum Opus.
Finishing the Magnum Opus wins the game outright, no matter what anyone else holds.

## 14 · Help, and your first goals

*No shot.*

**First edition.**
This pamphlet stays in the Compendium — the book button at the top of the screen — where bold words everywhere link to every rule in the game.
First goals: found a second city, finish a technology, build a worker, clear a barbarian camp.

**Second edition.**

## Pages the first edition does not have (candidates, ▢ the user)

Things the game has grown since 2026-09-03 that a first-time player meets in
the first hour and the pamphlet never mentions:

- **Your leader.** The four lines you chose on the new-game screen: two
  abilities from turn one, a unique unit and a unique building that arrive with
  their technologies. The Your Civ button on the dock shows what each is paying.
- **The wager.** When an age turns, three wagers are dealt; keep one and it pays
  a bead. This is where beads come from now, and page 13 should point at it.
- **Trade routes.** A trader carries a route between two cities; routes pay both
  ends. The book carries the detail, but the trader is the first unit a new
  player does not know what to do with.
- **Purchasing.** Gold and faith buy things outright from the city screen; a
  bought unit stands on the city hex.
- **Great people.** Renown calls them; a great person's work is planted on a hex
  and pays for the rest of the game.
- **The city under siege.** The portcullis on the banner, and why a besieged
  city heals slowly.
- **Ages.** The four ages, that your age is your own (it turns when you research
  into it), and what an age turn brings (the wager, your uniques).
