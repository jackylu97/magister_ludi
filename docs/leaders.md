# Leaders — figures for the seats (worksheet, 2026-09-10)

A worksheet in the `wager.md` shape. The game has no civ uniques today — a seat
is a colour and a charge (`heraldryFor`) — so a leader is a new system, and this
file is the casting call before the mechanics: who, why they are interesting,
which playstyle they fit, and one **signature** stated in the card vocabulary
the game already has, so it is one JSON row when the system exists. ▢ is the
user's; nothing here is ruled. The user: *"I know for sure i want to include
mithridates, king of pontus."*

Names already taken by great people (`data/greatPeople.json`) are avoided:
Imhotep, Hannibal, Boudica, Spartacus, Subutai, El Cid, Nzinga, Yi Sun-sin,
Leonardo and the rest of that roster stay great people.

**Conventions.** Signatures are deliberately modest (a doctrine's weight, not a
civ's) so a leader shades a playstyle rather than dictating it. Each fits one
of `docs/playstyles.md`'s families first and a sub-identity second.

## The one the user named

- **Mithridates VI of Pontus** (r. 120–63 BC) — the Poison King. Fought Rome
  three times over four decades, lost every war and came back from every one;
  spoke twenty-two languages; dosed himself with poisons until none could kill
  him (*mithridatism*). **Fit: wide imperium, the come-back.** Signature
  (rec): **The Antidote** — a malice seated against you expires one age early,
  and a captured city of yours costs its captor one more authority. The king
  you cannot finish. ▢

## Wide — the imperium

- **Pachacuti** (Inca, r. 1438–71) — the man who built the Qhapaq Ñan, forty
  thousand kilometres of road, and ruled through it. **Fit: roads are the
  spine.** Signature: domestic routes pave their road in half the time and a
  road-joined city pays one more happiness. ▢
- **Sargon of Akkad** (c. 2334–2279 BC) — the first empire, the first standing
  army, garrisons in every conquered city. **Fit: garrisons and puppets.**
  Signature: a garrisoned puppet demands no happiness. ▢
- **Basil II** (Byzantium, r. 976–1025) — the soldier-emperor who never married,
  campaigned every year for forty, and left a full treasury. **Fit: imperium
  with a hoard.** Signature: a soldier costs no upkeep while the treasury holds
  more than a bar. ▢
- **Tigranes the Great** (Armenia, r. 95–55 BC) — Mithridates' son-in-law and
  the King of Kings for a decade, who moved whole cities to fill his new
  capital. **Fit: imperium, the annexer.** Signature: annexing a city moves a
  citizen from it to your capital. ▢

## Wide — the steppe

- **Modu Chanyu** (Xiongnu, r. 209–174 BC) — the first steppe emperor, who
  trained his horsemen to shoot wherever his whistling arrow flew. **Fit: the
  horde.** Signature: mounted units heal fully on a hex they pillaged this
  turn. ▢
- **Tomyris** (Massagetae, 6th c. BC) — the queen who killed Cyrus the Great
  and, in Herodotus, put his head in a skin of blood. **Fit: steppe defence.**
  Signature: killing a unit inside your borders pays its cost in culture. ▢
- **Bumin Qaghan** (Göktürks, d. 552) — the blacksmith vassal who overthrew
  his overlords and founded the first Turkic khaganate. **Fit: steppe, the
  forge.** Signature: pastures pay production and iron is revealed a tech
  early. ▢

## Wide — land commerce

- **Sher Shah Suri** (r. 1540–45) — five years on the throne and he rebuilt the
  Grand Trunk Road, planted its trees, built its caravanserais and minted the
  rupee. **Fit: caravans, tolls, roads.** Signature: every Caravanserai pays
  gold for each road hex the city owns. ▢
- **Zenobia** (Palmyra, r. 267–72) — the caravan queen who took Egypt from Rome
  and was carried to Rome in golden chains. **Fit: land commerce that turns to
  war.** Signature: trade routes running through a city you captured this age
  pay double. ▢
- **Mansa Musa** (Mali, r. 1312–37) — famous, but the fit is exact: the
  richest man in history, whose pilgrimage crashed Cairo's gold price. **Fit:
  gold and faith together.** Signature: a proclamation pays gold to every city
  it reaches. ▢ (famous; the user may prefer lesser-known)
- **Amanirenas** (Kush, r. c. 40–10 BC) — the one-eyed kandake who fought
  Augustus to a treaty and kept his bronze head buried under her temple steps.
  **Fit: commerce with iron and a border held.** Signature: mines carrying a
  resource pay gold as well. ▢

## Wide — sea commerce

- **Rajendra Chola I** (r. 1014–44) — the only Indian king to send a fleet
  across the Bay of Bengal and take Srivijaya's ports. **Fit: the navy as
  commerce.** Signature: a foreign route ending in a city where your hull
  stands pays half again. ▢
- **Enrico Dandolo** (Venice, doge 1192–1205) — blind, past ninety, and he
  redirected a crusade to sack Constantinople for Venice's ledgers. **Fit:
  sea commerce, the entrepôt.** Signature: the host's gold from international
  routes doubles in your capital. ▢
- **Teuta** (Illyria, r. 231–227 BC) — the pirate queen who told Rome's envoys
  that piracy was a lawful trade. **Fit: sea raiding.** Signature: plundering a
  trade route pays its bounty twice, and your hulls take no counter-blow from
  a caravan. ▢
- **Hanno the Navigator** (Carthage, 5th c. BC) — sailed past the Pillars down
  the African coast and wrote it up on a temple wall. **Fit: exploration and
  the sea.** Signature: light hulls see two hexes further and claim ruins on
  the coast. ▢

## Tall — the library city

- **Al-Ma'mun** (Abbasid, r. 813–33) — the caliph of the House of Wisdom, who
  made a peace treaty's price a copy of the Almagest. **Fit: tall science by
  patronage.** Signature: a peace signed pays science, and the capital's
  science buildings pay per citizen. ▢
- **Ulugh Beg** (Timurid, r. 1447–49) — the astronomer-king of Samarkand whose
  star catalogue stood for two centuries, murdered by his son. **Fit: tall
  science, renaissance-punk.** Signature: the Observatory and Uraniborg pay
  renown, and a scholar great person costs a third less. ▢
- **Abd al-Rahman III** (Córdoba, r. 912–61) — half a million people, seventy
  libraries, a city the north sent its sons to study in. **Fit: the hub.**
  Signature: routes ending in your capital bring a copy of every luxury they
  pass. ▢

## Tall — the wonder city

- **Jayavarman VII** (Khmer, r. 1181–1218) — the builder of Angkor Thom, the
  Bayon and a hundred hospitals; the faces on the towers are his. **Fit: tall
  wonders and faith.** Signature: a wonder completed pays faith to every city,
  and the capital's rites last longer. ▢
- **Justinian and Theodora** (Byzantium, r. 527–65) — the Hagia Sophia, the
  Code, and the walls that held for a thousand years. **Fit: the walled tall
  city.** Signature: the capital's strength per citizen doubles and a law
  Order in the first slot pays twice. ▢
- **Djoser** (Egypt, c. 2670 BC) — the first pyramid, with Imhotep as his
  vizier (already a great person — the pair is the point). **Fit: the wonder
  city.** Signature: the first wonder of every age costs a quarter less. ▢

## Faith — the holy city

- **Akhenaten** (Egypt, r. c. 1353–36 BC) — invented a one-god religion, moved
  the capital to a new city in the desert, and was erased by his successors.
  **Fit: faith, the wandering court.** Signature: founding a religion also
  founds a holy city where your capital stands, and moving the capital is
  free once. ▢
- **Ezana of Aksum** (r. c. 320–60) — the first Christian king in Africa,
  whose obelisks still stand and whose coins carried the cross. **Fit: faith
  and Red Sea trade.** Signature: coins pay faith — every gold building
  supplies a little faith. ▢
- **Olga of Kiev** (r. 945–60) — avenged her husband four times over, then
  converted and became a saint. **Fit: faith by force.** Signature: a
  captured city converts to your religion, and its old faith's pressure is
  purged. ▢

## The occult and the late game (Æra IV/V flavour)

- **Rudolf II** (Holy Roman Emperor, r. 1576–1612) — the alchemists' emperor,
  patron of Kepler, Tycho and John Dee, keeper of the Wunderkammer, who let
  the empire run itself while he sought the Stone. **Fit: the Great Work.**
  Signature: the four stages cost less and the Opus door opens one bead early.
  ▢ (the late-game's natural leader; `docs/late-game.md`)
- **Ludovico Sforza** (Milan, r. 1494–99) — Leonardo's patron, who wanted the
  bronze horse and got the Last Supper. **Fit: renaissance-punk, the
  Artificer.** Signature: great people's works pay production and the
  Ornithopter comes a node early. ▢

## Notes for the system

- A leader is a seat's **persona** in the sim (`Player.persona` exists for
  bots) and a **charge and colour** in heraldry; the signature is one card
  effect on the seat, read by the same evaluator as a doctrine.
- Portraits are placeholders like everything visual; the heraldry canton
  carries the charge.
- ▢ how many at launch (rec: eight, one per sub-identity plus Mithridates and
  Rudolf), ▢ whether a leader locks a persona for bots (rec: yes — the
  spectator page then reads as a story).
