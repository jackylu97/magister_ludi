Instrument Serif is the skinny one — hairline-adjacent strokes, sharp modern cut, and its italic (the red Ludi) is the punchiest single move in the whole system. Fraunces is the thick-ligature energy — high optical size gives it heavy, soft-inked joins, like letterpress pushed too hard into paper. Swap either name into either face; they're set opposite here only for contrast.

Same three-voice rule as before — serifs speak, the sans works, the mono counts — but the whole ramp is now one family tree: Instrument Serif and Instrument Sans are siblings, Fraunces plays the heavyweight. No serif below ~15px, ever.

The board keeps its muted sage-and-wheat toon palette — that's scenery. The interface gets the saturated cuts: vermilion for the local player's actions, teal for the rival, cobalt/grape/gilt as the science/culture/gold voices. Bolder here means UI color is louder than terrain, never competing with it.

What changed from take one: ink borders 2px → 3px and shadows now solid ink (not translucent grey) at 6–8px throw — the "sticker on cardboard" read. A degree of rotation on cards and splashes so they sit like played pieces, not laid-out divs. The turn splash is the new loudest element: big skinny serif on hot vermilion — announcements get the display face and a full color block, everything else stays quiet so it lands.
