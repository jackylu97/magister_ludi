# The Scientific deck

**What it rewards:** Reaching things first. The deck has fewer raw bonuses and a slower start than the others, and in return it gets units, buildings and improvements before their age, versions nobody else can build, and rewards for finishing a technology before any other empire. Its science goes into **charter technologies**, which cost more research than ordinary ones and open the deck's strongest cards. **Directions inside it:** charter research (draft a charter, research its technology, then draw the cards it opens); early access to units, buildings and works; being first; institutions that mature (libraries, universities, academies, empire buildings); strategic resources and the mine; scholars and academies.

*Initially generated by `scripts/deckDocs.mjs`; now a hand-edited design spec (deck-list pass, 2026-10-06). **Built** means the effect exists in the game as written. **Revised** means the card exists in the data and this sheet changes it (the last column says what it was). **New** is a card this sheet proposes. **Deferred** means the card is in the data and not implemented. Tiers are proposed availability, not confirmed runtime unlocks.*

**Review (2026-10-06).** Target: about 45–55 seatable cards, with the charters counted separately. Before: 70 cards, of which 8 charters and 62 seatable. After the review: 43 cards, of which 8 charters and 35 seatable. The 27 cuts each fail a named test; none was cut for size. The reconciliation of the six sheets (2026-10-06) then cut The Scriptoria (Devotion's The Copying Houses keeps the idea), made Copying the Enemy a card shared with Dominion, renamed The Encyclopédie to The Charter Rolls, and restored Books from the Ships and Carried-Off Scholars as shared cards. That leaves 41 cards, of which 8 charters and 33 seatable. The balance pass (2026-10-06) changed no count: it excluded charter technologies from The Prize Essay and The Prize Question, raised The Presses to 6 gold a point with a ceiling, limited The Religious University to one Holy Site per University city, and made The Great Instrument reachable without winning the race to Alchemy. The deck now sits below the target because the first pass had several cards doing one job (five paying food or production on Academies, three paying for being first, two turning science into production, two paying science in Aqueduct cities) and several twins of cards on the Commerce, Dominion and Magisterial sheets. Its arcs:
- **Charters:** draft a Tier I charter, pay for its technology, seat what it opens; The Royal Charter Commission pays for finishing one, and The Charter Rolls and The Great Catalogue pay for holding many.
- **Institutions that mature:** The Long Study and The Old Academy, then Learned Foundations, Enduring Institutions and The Spectacle Makers, then The Long Inheritance, The Second Seat and The Collegium.
- **The military edge:** The Prospectors' Notes, The Assay and The Ordnance Rolls on strategic resources, then the Metallurgists', Mechanists' and Alchemists' charters and the cards behind them.
- **Being first** is a fourth, thin line: The Prize Essay, The Prize Question, The Great Instrument.

**How the two steps work.** A charter is drafted, never sits in a chair, and opens its technology for research the moment it is picked (unchanged behaviour). The technology costs more research than an ordinary one of its age and will sit in its own section of the tree. A card marked **Needs <technology>** in the card column is dealt only to a seat that knows that technology. That gate is a new mechanic for every such card (*needs: a draft gate on a known technology*), on top of whatever the card itself needs. A gated card may sit in a later tier than its charter when it is too strong to arrive early.

### Charter technologies

Each charter opens one technology. The technology gives early access by itself, and it opens the cards listed beside it.

| Charter (tier) | Technology | What knowing the technology gives | Cards it opens (tier) |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Glassmakers' Charter (I) | Optics | The Observatory before The Astrolabe, needing a Library rather than a University | The Temple Observatory (I), The Spectacle Makers (II) |
| The Surveyors' Charter (I) | Surveying | The Aqueduct before Engineering; the Qanat, an improvement that waters the farms beside it away from a river | The Field Survey (I) |
| The Schoolmen's Charter (I) | Scholasticism | The Cathedral and the Apostle before Theology | The Religious University (II) |
| The Metallurgists' Charter (II) | Alloys | The Crucible Guard, a unit only empires knowing Alloys can train; the Forge before Steel | Crucible Steel (II) |
| The Naturalists' Charter (II) | Taxonomy | +1 food on your worked plantations and pastures | New Crops (II) |
| The Mechanists' Charter (II) | Mechanics | The Trebuchet and the Crossbowman before their technologies | The Siege College (II), The Engine Works (III) |
| The Printers' Charter (II) | Printing | The Printing House before Movable Type; +1 trade route | The Presses (III), The Charter Rolls (III) |
| The Alchemists' Charter (III) | Chemistry | The Fire Lance before Alchemy (Chemistry does not open the Magnum Opus) | The Saltpetre Works (III) |

None of these technologies exist in `data/techs.json` yet. A technology can already unlock units and buildings and carry card effects (Steel and Machinery do), so a charter does not need to unlock a unit itself. What is new: the technologies, a second way into an existing row (the Aqueduct, the Observatory and the others opened early), and the Qanat and Crucible Guard rows.

**Is each charter worth its extra research?** (review)
- **Glassmakers':** yes, on its own. An Observatory an age early is the deck's best science, and The Spectacle Makers speeds up every maturity card.
- **Surveyors':** yes with Expansionist (an early Aqueduct is growth); alone it is an early Aqueduct and one card. The Qanat here and Expansionist's Qanats card are the same improvement, so one row should serve both.
- **Schoolmen's:** only with Devotion. With The Scriptoria cut in the reconciliation, it opens The Religious University alone, which pays only where Holy Sites stand.
- **Metallurgists':** yes. A unit nobody else can train, the Forge early, and Crucible Steel.
- **Naturalists':** the weakest. Its own effect is a raw food bonus and New Crops is a plantation bonus, so it is close to the raw bonus behind a charter that the spec warns against. It earns its price only on a plantation-heavy map. If playtests show nobody takes it, rethink it rather than add cards behind it. Husbandry Studies was cut from behind it as weak.
- **Mechanists':** yes. Siege a full age early, and two cards behind it.
- **Printers':** yes. An extra trade route, and The Presses, one of the strongest cards behind any charter.
- **Alchemists':** yes for the Fire Lance alone. Its one card (The Saltpetre Works) is thin; The Apothecaries was cut from behind it as weak.

## Synergies with other decks

### Dominion — the military edge
- **Scientific gives Dominion** units early, or units nobody else has: the Crucible Guard (Alloys), the Trebuchet and Crossbowman a full age early (Mechanics), and the Fire Lance before Alchemy (Chemistry).
- **Dominion gives Scientific** science from fighting and captured cities with their libraries and universities. Dominion takes ground while the expensive charter technologies come in, which covers Scientific's slow tempo. Science from captured cities is the shared Carried-Off Scholars.
- **Hooks this sheet creates:** units ahead of their age; extra strategic copies (The Ordnance Rolls). **Hooks this sheet reads:** kills (Field Academies, Copying the Enemy) and captured cities (Carried-Off Scholars).
- **Shared improvements:** strategic resources on mines, pastures and camps. Scientific pays science and extra copies on them; Dominion builds its army on them.
- **Cards that serve the pair:** The Metallurgists' Charter, Crucible Steel, The Mechanists' Charter, The Siege College, The Alchemists' Charter, The Saltpetre Works, The Ordnance Rolls, The Assay, Copying the Enemy, Carried-Off Scholars, Field Academies, The Engineer Corps.

### Expansionist — the engineers
- **Scientific gives Expansionist** improvements and public works ahead of their age: the Aqueduct and the Qanat (Surveying), and more food on plantations and pastures (Taxonomy).
- **Expansionist gives Scientific** the terrain and the population its science comes from, and growth that covers Scientific's slow tempo. This is the pair that covers a gap most directly: Expansionist's science.
- **Hooks this sheet creates:** improvements ahead of their age (Qanat, Aqueduct). **Hooks this sheet reads:** population (The Scribes' Hall, Hydraulic Schools), worked land (The Field Survey), buried seams (The Prospectors' Notes).
- **Shared improvements:** mines and quarries, where Scientific pays science (The Field Survey, The Exchange of Discoveries) and Expansionist pays production; plantations and farms through New Crops (a farm beside a plantation, close to Expansionist's Three Sisters).
- **Cards that serve the pair:** The Surveyors' Charter, The Field Survey, The Naturalists' Charter, New Crops, The Scribes' Hall, The Prospectors' Notes, Hydraulic Schools.

### Commerce — the patrons
- **Scientific gives Commerce** trade unlocks ahead of their age: Printing gives an extra trade route and the Printing House early.
- **Commerce gives Scientific** gold that buys libraries and universities (covering Scientific's tempo), gold that buys research directly, and foreign routes that pay science.
- **Hooks this sheet creates:** Printing's extra route. **Hooks this sheet reads:** gold (The Presses), foreign routes (Books from the Ships, Merchant Scholars, The Merchant Navigators), Markets (The Exchange of Discoveries).
- **Shared improvements:** mines, quarries and plantations, the most overlap of any pair. Scientific pays science on them (The Field Survey, New Crops, The Exchange of Discoveries); Commerce pays gold. The Assay lost its gold for this reason.
- **Exploration:** Commerce's theme, with its own cards there. Scientific keeps only Field Notebooks, for light overlap.
- **Cards that serve the pair:** The Printers' Charter, The Presses, Books from the Ships, Merchant Scholars, The Merchant Navigators, The Exchange of Discoveries, Field Notebooks.

### Devotion — scholars of the faith
- **Scientific gives Devotion** religious unlocks ahead of their age: the Cathedral and the Apostle before Theology (Scholasticism).
- **Devotion gives Scientific** faith that buys science buildings and keeps research moving (Devotion's The Copying Houses), and temples that teach.
- **Hooks this sheet creates:** early religious buildings and units. **Hooks this sheet reads:** faith (The Cloister), Temples (The Temple Observatory, The Monastery Libraries), Holy Sites (The Religious University).
- **Shared improvements:** holy sites beside academies. The Religious University makes one Holy Site in each University city count as an Academy, so Scientific's academy cards pay on Devotion's work. Plantations (New Crops) are now shared only behind the Naturalists' Charter.
- **Cards that serve the pair:** The Schoolmen's Charter, The Religious University, The Glassmakers' Charter, The Temple Observatory, Cloister Schools, The Cloister, The Monastery Libraries.

### Magisterial — the academy
- **Scientific gives Magisterial** charters, which are drafted and never take a chair, so they count as draft actions; and scholars and academies for Magisterial's any-family cards.
- **Magisterial gives Scientific** more drafts and rerolls, which means more charters seen and a better chance of finding the charter it needs.
- **Hooks this sheet creates:** charters drafted (counted by The Charter Rolls) and charter technologies known (The Great Catalogue); scholars (The Royal Charter Commission, The Collegium). **Hooks this sheet reads:** none of Magisterial's directly; more drafts simply show more charters. Larger hands live on Magisterial's sheet (Officials by Merit).
- **Shared improvements:** academies and landmarks (the shared The House of Wisdom).
- **Cards that serve the pair:** The Charter Rolls, The Great Catalogue, The Royal Charter Commission, Discoveries through Scholars, The House of Wisdom, Illuminated Treatises, and every charter.

## Shared cards for the user to rule on

Only one of the two sheets recommends a change to these, so each stays on both shelves until the user rules.
- **Hydraulic Schools** (with Expansionist): Expansionist recommends cutting it from its own shelf and leaving it Scientific's alone, because its science stacks two conditions (an Aqueduct and a mountain) and science beside a mountain is The Mountain Schools' job there. Scientific does not comment.
- **Merchant Scholars** (with Commerce): Scientific recommends keeping it but narrowing it to count Academies rather than every family's works, since this deck's great-person cards read Academies only. Commerce does not comment.
- **The Engineer Corps** (with Dominion): Scientific recommends cutting it, or cutting it to its first clause, because its three clauses are unrelated and its faster siege production repeats Field Academies. Dominion keeps it.
- **The Monastery Libraries** (with Devotion): Devotion recommends cutting it, because it is a flat line on two buildings every city builds and Cloister Schools already rewards a Temple beside a Library. Scientific does not comment.
- **Illuminated Treatises** (with Magisterial): Magisterial recommends cutting it, because flat yields behind a two-building gate change no decision. Scientific does not comment.
