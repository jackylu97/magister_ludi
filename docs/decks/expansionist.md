# The Expansionist deck

**What it rewards:** The map as the engine. You raise, tend and claim land, and the empire is as strong as its ground is good and wide. The deck raises the two limits on growth every other deck runs into: authority (how many towns you can hold) and happiness (how big they can grow). It is weak at science and culture and gets them only from particular terrain: science from mountains and hills, culture from old forest, wild land and coast. **Directions inside it:** the field (farms, lumber mills, mines and quarries, and tiles raised over time); the managed wild (tended land and the wild-land bonuses); the wide empire (claims, outposts and authority relief earned by developed towns); the tall empire (food, water and contentment); levies (citizens spent on production and soldiers); public works (engineers, manufactories, aqueducts and irrigation).

*Initially generated by `scripts/deckDocs.mjs`; now a hand-edited design spec (deck-list pass, 2026-10-06). **Built** means the effect exists in the game as written, **revised** means this sheet changes a live effect (the last column says what it was), **new** means a card proposed in this pass (the last column says whether existing effects can express it or what it needs), and **deferred** means the card is in the data but its effect is not implemented. A deferred card whose text this sheet changes stays **deferred**, and its last column starts with "Was: …". Government tiers here are proposed availability, not confirmation of runtime unlocks. The generator runs only on request and overwrites these edits.*

**Words.** Rules text on this sheet says **wild** (no improvement: untouched or tended), **tended** (one of the tended works below) and **improved** (an ordinary improvement). Deferred and new cards say "wild" where the old text said "unimproved"; that is a change of word only and is not marked. Built cards still print "unimproved". The meaning is the same, because a tended work is not an improvement, so tended tiles will count for those cards as soon as tended works exist.

**Tended land on this sheet.** Each tended work is unlocked by its own card, so the draft decides which ones a game has. A worker tends a tile once, for one charge; burning is a town's decision, made once per town on a cycle. All tending is permanent and survives pillage. Tended works open no resources. First-pass yields, set so that a farm on fresh water stays ahead on raw food:

| Work | Unlocked by | Where | Yield |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forest garden | The Forest Gardens (Tier 0) | Forest or jungle; the forest stays standing | +1 food |
| Fish traps | The Fish Traps (Tier 0) | River tiles and lakeshores | +1 food, +1 production |
| Root-crop grounds | The Root Grounds (Tier I) | Grassland | +1 food |
| Wild orchard | The Wild Orchards (Tier I) | Hills or forest | +1 food, +1 gold |
| Burned country | The Burning Season (Tier I) | A town's wild grassland and plains, all at once | +1 food, plus the season's cycle |
| Fertilised ground | Fertile Ground (Tier II) | Any land tile, under whatever stands on it | +1 food |

Later cards pay off having tended early: The Tended Country, The Keepers of Country, The Landscape Garden (Tier II–III), The Long Tending (Tier III) and The Garden of the World (Tier IV).

## Synergies with other decks

Each pair below follows the matching entry in `docs/decks/deck-design.md` ("What pairs of decks should enable"). A **hook** is something one deck's cards create that the other deck's cards read. This sheet creates its hooks whether or not the partner is seated. Expansionist's currency is food and land; the test for each pair is whether that currency feeds the partner's way of spending, and the reverse.

### Expansionist and Dominion: the frontier
- **Expansionist gives Dominion** levies: citizens turned into soldiers, so a big empire raises an army without spending production. Also new towns brought up to strength quickly.
- **Expansionist gets back** cleared ground. Burnt camps and captured towns leave land to claim and towns to hold.
- **Hooks this sheet creates:** levied units (The Muster of the Fields), newly founded towns (The Seed Grain, Daughter Towns, The Daughter Province), claimed land (Claim by the Spade, Outposts, The Barbarian Frontier) and towns connected to the capital (Provincial Councils).
- **Hooks this sheet reads:** cleared camps (The Barbarian Frontier, The Wide Country) and units standing in towns (Veteran Colonies, the shared Frontier Garrisons).
- **Shared improvements:** mines on strategic resources, and pastures. Expansionist pays production on them (The Prospectors, The Engineers' Yards, The Steppe); Dominion counts them for its army.
- **Cards on this sheet that serve the pair:** The Muster of the Fields, Levy of the Land, Seasonal Levies, Veteran Colonies (veteran colonies), The Seed Grain, Provincial Councils, The Steppe, The Woodsmen's March, The Wide Country, The Patient Harvest (as its cost), and the shared The Barbarian Frontier, Frontier Garrisons, Military Colonies and Marcher Lords.
- **Left for Dominion's sheet:** soldier-farmers (garrisons that farm the land around them), satrapies (a conquered city that is cheaper to hold but pays less) and relay stations (connected cities that share sight or speed units). All three read Dominion's garrisons and captures first. Veteran Colonies is on this sheet because it pays in citizens; Dominion may want to share it.

### Expansionist and Commerce: the land and the market
- **Expansionist gives Commerce** goods to sell: claimed and improved land opens resources, prospecting surfaces seams, and the breadbasket's surplus food can be carried away.
- **Expansionist gets back** purchases: gold buys tiles, workers and settlers, so the empire grows faster than food alone allows. Commerce's foreign trade routes are also the pair's cover for this deck's science.
- **Hooks this sheet creates:** opened resources (The Pioneers' Portion, Claim by the Spade), surfaced seams (The Prospectors), surplus food in towns that have stopped growing (The Breadbasket), and connected towns (Provincial Councils).
- **Hooks this sheet reads:** gold spent on tiles, workers and settlers (The Wide Country counts bought hexes), roads (Market Gardens) and domestic trade routes (The Grain Exchange, The Provincial Fair).
- **Shared improvements:** mines and quarries. Expansionist pays production on them (The Highlands, The Prospectors, The Engineers' Yards); Commerce pays gold.
- **Cards on this sheet that serve the pair:** Smallholdings, The Prospectors, The Breadbasket, The Grand Canal (canals), The Pioneers' Portion, The Wide Country, and the shared Market Gardens, The Grain Exchange (the grain supply) and The Provincial Fair.
- **Left for Commerce's sheet:** market towns and fairs (a town where routes meet) and tiles along a trade route growing richer. Both read trade routes first. Commerce's sheet already carries them (The Home Market, The Waystations). Canals are carded here as a public work.

### Expansionist and Devotion: wild land and faith
- **Expansionist gives Devotion** wild land and population: tended and wild land for Devotion's cards to pay faith on, and big towns that hold more followers.
- **Expansionist gets back** happiness and miracles: faith that grows towns and keeps them content.
- **Hooks this sheet creates:** tended and wild tiles (every tended-work card, The Commons, The Old Ways), burning seasons (The Burning Season, which a Devotion card could turn into a festival that pays faith), and large towns (The Grain Dole, The Full Granaries).
- **Hooks this sheet reads:** shrines (The Groves and the Stones), towns following your religion (the shared Harvest Offerings).
- **Shared improvements:** none on the family lists. The two meet on wild land: Devotion's camps and this deck's tended works are both land kept wild on purpose. Pastures are the fallback; Smallholdings and The Steppe already pay food, gold and production on them.
- **Cards on this sheet that serve the pair:** The Groves and the Stones, Forest Sanctuaries, The Commons, The Burning Season, The Land Shapes Us (its desert trait pays faith), and the shared Sacred Groves (sacred groves) and Harvest Offerings.
- **Left for Devotion's sheet:** temple estates (a holy site that raises the land around it), monks who clear the frontier (religious units that tend or claim land; they would read this sheet's tended works), songlines (sacred sites connected across the land) and the flood festival (a town's burning season that also pays faith; it reads The Burning Season).

### Expansionist and Scientific: the engineers
- **Expansionist gives Scientific** the terrain science comes from (mountains, hills) and a large population to work libraries and universities.
- **Expansionist gets back** improvements and public works ahead of their age, from Scientific's charters. This pair covers Expansionist's science gap most directly of the fifteen.
- **Hooks this sheet creates:** raised tiles (Dark Earth, The Raised Fields, Fertile Ground), mines and quarries on hills and beside mountains (The Highlands, The Mountain Schools), aqueduct towns (The Waterworks) and population.
- **Hooks this sheet reads:** public works a Scientific charter could unlock early: Irrigation Works, Qanats, The Grand Canal and Aqueducts (The Waterworks, Public Works).
- **Shared improvements:** mines and quarries. Expansionist pays production and, on hills and beside mountains, science; Scientific's own cards pay science on resource tiles.
- **Cards on this sheet that serve the pair:** The Naturalists, The High Pastures, The Highlands, The Mountain Schools, Qanats (underground channels), Irrigation Works, The Waterworks, Public Works, and the shared Hydraulic Schools.
- **Left for Scientific's sheet:** new crops (a technology that raises a kind of tile everywhere), surveying (science from working land) and writing for grain (science from stored food). Each pays science from something other than terrain, which this deck's gap rule forbids; on Scientific's sheet they read this deck's hooks. The Hydraulic Commonwealth, which pays science from food, is writing for grain; it has moved to Scientific's sheet alone (see the note under Proposed cuts).

### Expansionist and Magisterial: shaping territory
- **Expansionist gives Magisterial** room: claimed and tended land to place works on, laid out the way Magisterial's cards reward.
- **Expansionist gets back** culture and territory: great-person works that claim land around them, and boons that supply what the land does not.
- **Hooks this sheet creates:** tended works, which could count toward Magisterial's arrangements; manufactories (The Engineers' Yards, The Standing Patronage, A Court of Engineers); and claimed land.
- **Hooks this sheet reads:** great-person works and wonders (The Hermitages, The Landscape Garden, the shared The Sculpted Landscape) and Monuments (the shared Garden Provinces and Garden Cities).
- **Shared improvements:** manufactories, and landmarks beside tended land.
- **Cards on this sheet that serve the pair:** The Landscape Garden (landscape gardens), The Hermitages, The Engineers' Yards, The Standing Patronage, A Court of Engineers, Public Works, and the shared Garden Provinces, Garden Cities and The Sculpted Landscape.
- **Left for Magisterial's sheet:** the four-part garden (works laid out in a pattern pay more) and siting by the land (a work that pays for what surrounds it). Both are arrangement cards, Magisterial's theme. They should let a tended tile count as part of an arrangement. The Quarter of Masters, cut below, is an arrangement card Magisterial could take.
