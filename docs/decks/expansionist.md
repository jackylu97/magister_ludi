# The Expansionist deck

**What it rewards:** The map as the engine. You raise, tend and claim land, and the empire is as strong as its ground is good and wide. The deck raises the two limits on growth every other deck runs into: authority (how many towns you can hold) and happiness (how big they can grow). It is weak at science and culture and gets them only from particular terrain: science from mountains and hills, culture from old forest, wild land and coast. **Directions inside it:** the field (farms, lumber mills, mines and quarries, and tiles raised over time); the managed wild (tended land and the wild-land bonuses); the wide empire (claims, outposts and authority relief earned by developed towns); the tall empire (food, water and contentment); levies (citizens spent on production and soldiers); public works (engineers, manufactories, aqueducts and irrigation).

**Words.** Rules text on this sheet says **wild** (no improvement: untouched or tended), **tended** (one of the tended works below) and **improved** (an ordinary improvement). Deferred and new cards say "wild" where the old text said "unimproved"; that is a change of word only and is not marked. Built cards still print "unimproved". The meaning is the same, because a tended work is not an improvement, so tended tiles will count for those cards as soon as tended works exist.

**Tended land on this sheet.** Each tended work is unlocked by its own card, so the draft decides which ones a game has. A worker tends a tile once, for one charge; burning is a town's decision, made once per town on a cycle. All tending is permanent and survives pillage. Tended works open no resources. First-pass yields, set so that a farm on fresh water stays ahead on raw food:

| Work | Unlocked by | Where | Yield |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forest garden | The Forest Gardens (Tier 0) | Forest or jungle; the forest stays standing | +1 food |
| Fish traps | The Fish Traps (Tier 0) | Lake or coastal tiles | +1 food, +1 production |
| Wild orchard | The Wild Orchards (Tier I) | Forest or jungle | +1 food, +1 gold |
| Burned country | The Burning Season (Tier I) | A town's wild grassland and plains, all at once | +1 food, plus the season's cycle |

The arc: the four unlocks above (Tiers 0–I); The Commons, The Old Songs and Six Gardens to build up (Tier I); The Tended Country and The Landscape Garden in the middle (Tier II); and two payoffs for having tended early, The Long Tending (Tier III) and The Garden of the World (Tier IV). Root-crop grounds and fertilised ground were cut in the review (see Proposed cuts). Since the balance pass, each unlock is drafted once and takes no chair, and the payoff cards marked **Needs tended land** are dealt only to a seat holding at least one unlock.

### Tended works, as built (D3)

- Each tended work is an ordinary improvement row in `data/improvements.json` marked `tended`: Forest Garden (forest or jungle, +1 food), Fish Traps (coast or lake water, +1 food +1 production), Wild Orchard (forest or jungle, +1 food +1 gold) and Burned Country (wild grassland or plains, +1 food). A worker builds one for one charge through the ordinary build action.
- Each is opened by its own charter (`CharterSpec.unlocks.improvement`): the card is drafted once, takes no chair, and stays in the collection. A worker's menu hides a tended work until its card is held.
- A tended hex counts as wild for every card (`unimproved` holds, `improved` does not) and answers the new hex test `tended`.
- A tended work cannot be pillaged (a road on the same hex still can). Felling the forest under a forest garden or wild orchard removes it.
- Tended works open no resource. On a hex whose resource wants its own improvement, the ordinary rule still applies ("the deer wants a camp").
- The turn a tended work went up is recorded the same way as any improvement's, which The Long Tending and The Inheritance of Fields read.
- Cards marked **Needs tended land** carry `needsTendedWork` and are dealt only to a seat holding one of the four unlocks.
- Placeholder art: a garden and an orchard reuse the plantation's rows, the traps reuse the fishing boat, and burned country reuses the farm's furrows in a darker shade.

## Synergies with other decks

Each pair below follows the matching entry in `docs/decks/deck-design.md` ("What pairs of decks should enable"). A **hook** is something one deck's cards create that the other deck's cards read. This sheet creates its hooks whether or not the partner is seated. Expansionist's currency is food and land; the test for each pair is whether that currency feeds the partner's way of spending, and the reverse.

### Expansionist and Dominion: the frontier
- **Expansionist gives Dominion** levies: citizens turned into soldiers, so a big empire raises an army without spending production. Also new towns brought up to strength quickly.
- **Expansionist gets back** cleared ground. Burnt camps and captured towns leave land to claim and towns to hold.
- **Hooks this sheet creates:** levied units that count as trained (The Muster of the Fields), newly founded towns (The Seed Grain, Daughter Towns), claimed land (Outposts, The Barbarian Frontier) and towns connected to the capital (Provincial Councils).
- **Hooks this sheet reads:** cleared camps (The Barbarian Frontier, The Wide Country) and units standing in towns (Veteran Colonies, the shared The Road Garrisons).
- **Shared improvements:** mines on strategic resources. Expansionist pays production on them (The Prospectors, The Engineers' Yards); Dominion counts them for its army.
- **Cards on this sheet that serve the pair:** The Muster of the Fields, Levy of the Land, Seasonal Levies, Veteran Colonies (veteran colonies), The Seed Grain, Daughter Towns, Provincial Councils, The Wide Country, The General Levy (a citizen army for the late war; its units do not count as trained), The Patient Harvest (as its cost), and the shared The Barbarian Frontier, The Road Garrisons, Military Colonies and Marcher Lords.
- **Left for Dominion's sheet:** soldier-farmers (garrisons that farm the land around them), satrapies (a conquered city that is cheaper to hold but pays less) and relay stations (connected cities that share sight or speed units). All three read Dominion's garrisons and captures first. The Steppe (pastures and cheaper mounted units) and The Woodsmen's March (combat in forest and hills) were cut here as Dominion's kind of card; Dominion's sheet may take The Steppe.

### Expansionist and Commerce: the land and the market
- **Expansionist gives Commerce** goods to sell: claimed and improved land opens resources, prospecting surfaces seams, and the breadbasket's surplus food can be carried away.
- **Expansionist gets back** purchases: gold buys tiles, workers and settlers, so the empire grows faster than food alone allows. Commerce's foreign trade routes are also the pair's cover for this deck's science.
- **Hooks this sheet creates:** claimed land that opens resources (Outposts), surfaced seams (The Prospectors), surplus food in towns that have stopped growing (The Breadbasket; the shared The Grain Exchange carries it away), and connected towns (Provincial Councils).
- **Hooks this sheet reads:** gold spent on tiles, workers and settlers (The Wide Country counts bought hexes), roads (Market Gardens) and domestic trade routes (The Grain Exchange, The Provincial Fair).
- **Shared improvements:** mines and quarries. Expansionist pays production on them (The Highlands, The Prospectors, The Engineers' Yards); Commerce pays gold.
- **Cards on this sheet that serve the pair:** Smallholdings, The Prospectors, The Breadbasket, Outposts, The Wide Country, and the shared Market Gardens, The Grain Exchange (the grain supply) and The Provincial Fair.
- **Left for Commerce's sheet:** market towns and fairs (a town where routes meet) and tiles along a trade route growing richer. Both read trade routes first. Commerce's sheet carries market towns (The Home Market); its card for tiles along a route (The Waystations) was cut there. Canals were carded here as The Grand Canal and cut in review: a new improvement that ships can enter is a larger decision than a card, as Commerce's sheet also says.

### Expansionist and Devotion: wild land and faith
- **Expansionist gives Devotion** wild land and population: tended and wild land for Devotion's cards to pay faith on, and big towns that hold more followers.
- **Expansionist gets back** happiness and miracles: faith that grows towns and keeps them content.
- **Hooks this sheet creates:** tended and wild tiles (every tended-work card, The Commons, The Old Songs), burning seasons (The Burning Season, which the shared The Flood Festival turns into faith), and large towns (The Grain Dole).
- **Hooks this sheet reads:** wild forest kept for faith (the shared Sacred Groves).
- **Shared improvements:** none on the family lists. The two meet on wild land: Devotion's camps and this deck's tended works are both land kept wild on purpose. Pastures are the fallback; Smallholdings and The Three Sisters already pay on them.
- **Cards on this sheet that serve the pair:** The Commons, The Burning Season, The Old Songs, The Spirits of Place (faith from wild land), and the shared Sacred Groves (sacred groves) and The Flood Festival (the flood festival).
- **Left for Devotion's sheet:** temple estates (a holy site that raises the land around it), monks who clear the frontier (religious units that tend or claim land; they would read this sheet's tended works), and songlines (sacred sites connected across the land). The flood festival is now the shared The Flood Festival, carded on both sheets. The Groves and the Stones and Forest Sanctuaries, which paid faith on forest here, were cut: faith on wild forest is the shared Sacred Groves' job.

### Expansionist and Scientific: the engineers
- **Expansionist gives Scientific** the terrain science comes from (mountains, hills) and a large population to work libraries and universities.
- **Expansionist gets back** improvements and public works ahead of their age, from Scientific's charters. This pair covers Expansionist's science gap most directly of the fifteen.
- **Hooks this sheet creates:** raised tiles (Dark Earth, The Three Sisters), mines and quarries on hills and beside mountains (The Highlands, The Mountain Schools), aqueduct towns (The Waterworks), farms that pass water to their neighbours (The Watercourses) and population.
- **Hooks this sheet reads:** public works a Scientific charter unlocks early: the Qanat, which waters the farms beside it by the same rule The Watercourses needs, and the Aqueduct (The Waterworks, Public Works).
- **Shared improvements:** mines and quarries. Expansionist pays production and, on hills and beside mountains, science; Scientific's own cards pay science on resource tiles.
- **Cards on this sheet that serve the pair:** The Naturalists, The Highlands, The Mountain Schools, The Philosophers' Peaks, The Watercourses, The Waterworks, Public Works, and the shared Hydraulic Schools (kept by the user, 2026-10-12).
- **Left for Scientific's sheet:** new crops (a technology that raises a kind of tile everywhere), surveying (science from working land) and writing for grain (science from stored food). Each pays science from something other than terrain, which this deck's gap rule forbids; on Scientific's sheet they read this deck's hooks. The Hydraulic Commonwealth went to Scientific's sheet, whose review then cut it (see the note under Proposed cuts). Qanats was cut here in review because Scientific's The Surveyors' Charter already unlocks the Qanat.

### Expansionist and Magisterial: shaping territory
- **Expansionist gives Magisterial** room: claimed and tended land to place works on, laid out the way Magisterial's cards reward.
- **Expansionist gets back** culture and territory: great-person works that claim land around them, and boons that supply what the land does not.
- **Hooks this sheet creates:** tended works, which Magisterial's Commission: The Paradise Garden counts as sides of a garden; manufactories and great engineers (The Master Builders, The Engineers' Yards, A Court of Engineers, The Master of Works, The Raising of the Land); and claimed land (Outposts, The Beating of the Bounds).
- **Hooks this sheet reads:** great-person works and wonders (The Landscape Garden, the shared The Sculpted Landscape) and Monuments (the shared Garden Provinces and Garden Cities).
- **Shared improvements:** manufactories, and landmarks beside tended land.
- **Cards on this sheet that serve the pair:** The Landscape Garden (landscape gardens), The Master Builders, The Engineers' Yards, A Court of Engineers, The Master of Works, The Raising of the Land, The Domesday Survey (its reward is a great engineer), and the shared Garden Provinces, Garden Cities and The Sculpted Landscape.
- **Left for Magisterial's sheet:** the four-part garden (carded there as Commission: The Paradise Garden) and siting by the land (a work that pays for what surrounds it; carded there as The Siting Masters). Both are arrangement cards, Magisterial's theme. The Quarter of Masters went to Magisterial in the first pass, and The Hermitages (great-person works that count as wild) was cut here in review.
